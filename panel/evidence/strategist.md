# Evidence: The Strategist

Last refreshed: 2026-10-03

Evidence basis: Search summaries only. About 26 WebSearch queries were run; every result came back as a title, URL and short summary. I tried WebFetch on 9 pages (frontlinegaming.org, boardgamequest.com, tabletopgaming.co.uk, gideonsgaming.com, opinionatedgamers.com, fuzzyllamareviews.com) and all were blocked by the egress proxy, so I opened no page in full. The sources below are therefore what the search summaries said about each page, not my own reading of the full articles. BoardGameGeek, Reddit and Meeple Mountain were blocked and are not used as opened sources. Persona caveat: most of these are reviewer and blogger voices, not forum posts, so they under-represent raw community complaints. No ratings, counts or sales figures were found, and none are given.

## Recurring themes

1. **Players want decisions that feel like their own; output randomness that undoes a plan is resented, input randomness is tolerated.**
   - Type: complaint (about luck that overrides skill), with praise for randomness that arrives before the decision.
   - Frequency: often (about 5 sources; also surfaced in the game reactions for Flip 7, Port Royal and 7 Wonders).
   - URLs: https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/features/do-euros-feel-luck-punk-a-cardboard-manifesto ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-role-of-luck-why-rng-isn-t-the-answer ; https://zatu.com/blogs/reviews/flip-7-second-opinion ; https://boardgamereview.co.uk/game-reviews/flip-7-card-game-review/ ; https://miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/port-royal-review

2. **"Multiplayer solitaire" is a common knock on engine builders and tableau games.**
   - Type: complaint.
   - Frequency: often (about 4 sources; applied to Wingspan, Splendor and 7 Wonders).
   - URLs: https://www.nerdly.co.uk/2020/02/27/wingspan-board-game-review/ ; https://frontlinegaming.org/2025/05/09/board-game-review-splendor/ ; https://statelyplay.com/2017/11/14/review-7-wonders/ ; https://cultofthenew.com/2011/04/05/7-wonders/

## Reactions to well-known games

All reactions below come from search summaries only. Ratings: not found for every game.

- **Flip 7.** Strategy-minded readers see it as almost pure luck with a thin layer of probability and some card tracking. Action cards are what keep it interesting. Several reviews say it is a fun, quick "gateway" or non-gamer game, and that anyone hoping to outsmart the deck will be annoyed. Strategic payoff exists but is small, and busting several rounds in a row frustrates. Strategist verdict: tolerable as a warm-up, not a game to plan around. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://boardgamereview.co.uk/game-reviews/flip-7-card-game-review/ ; https://zatu.com/blogs/reviews/flip-7-second-opinion ; https://hexagamers.com/flip-7-review/ ; https://whatsericplaying.com/2025/02/24/flip-7/ ; https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260110-flip-7-review

- **Port Royal.** Praised for tidy push-your-luck with light engine building and a good balance of crew costs and points. Strategists note luck still dominates, and the push-your-luck can swing wildly when a player has many sailors or pirates. The tension of how much to reveal for opponents is the main skill element. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/port-royal-review ; https://www.meeplelikeus.co.uk/port-royal-2014/ ; https://zatu.com/blogs/reviews/port-royal-review ; https://boardgamequest.com/port-royal-review

- **Sea Salt & Paper.** Reviewers describe layered choices (discard-pile reading, colour and set collection, when to call stop) that suit lighter strategy players. At two players it becomes a duel over the two discard piles, and one review said stopping early or pushing late both felt like chosen decisions. Complaints are the instant-win mermaid case and a runaway-leader feel at low player counts. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://hexagamers.com/sea-salt-and-paper-review/ ; https://therewillbe.games/articles-boardgame-reviews/9424-push-it-real-good-your-luck-a-sea-salt-and-paper-board-game-review ; https://whatsericplaying.com/2023/09/18/sea-salt-paper/ ; https://thefandomentals.com/sea-salt-and-paper-review

- **Wingspan.** Widely praised for a friendly, visible engine and for art and theme. The recurring complaint from heavier players is that it is close to multiplayer solitaire with little competition, and that it is a puzzle over a random bird carousel. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://www.nerdly.co.uk/2020/02/27/wingspan-board-game-review/ ; https://opinionatedgamers.com/2019/01/27/wingspan/ ; https://www.wargamer.com/wingspan/review ; https://www.boardseyeview.net/post/2019/02/24/wingspan

- **Pandemic.** Valued as a cooperative puzzle, but the best-known complaint is quarterbacking: with open information, one dominant player can effectively solve it for the group. Some argue it is a people problem; hiding hands as the rules say is cited as a fix. Pandemic Legacy was noted as lacking replay once the story is known. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://gideonsgaming.com/board-game-quarterbacking-player-problem-or-game-problem/ ; https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/quarterbacking-in-boardgames-a-pandemic-problem-or-a-people-problem/151473 ; https://airborneham.substack.com/p/alpha-gamers-cooperative-games-and ; https://zatu.com/en-us/blogs/reviews/pandemic-legacy-season-one-review

- **Azul.** Praised as a sharp, accessible tile-drafting puzzle with denial and timing. Strategists say it lacks long-term depth and that it takes a few plays to see the strategy. Opinion splits on player count: some find two players most strategic and chess-like, others prefer four. Summer Pavilion was described as more tactical but slower. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://frontlinegaming.org/2025/06/25/board-game-review-azul/ ; https://www.gamesradar.com/azul-board-game-review/ ; https://sites.miamioh.edu/tabletop/2020/05/game-review-azul/ ; https://tbgd.blog/2019/12/12/azul-summer-pavilion-review/ ; https://lemm.ee/post/68053/336341

- **Brass: Birmingham.** Search summaries describe it as a top-tier heavy economic game rewarding planning, adaptation and tight competition, with shared networks that tie players together. New players need a few rounds to find their footing, and loans are a common first-play hesitation. My first search for it returned only brass-band results, so the evidence is thinner than for others, and I found no explicit complaints beyond the learning curve. Rating: one retailer figure of 4.7 stars appeared in a summary, which I do not treat as reliable; BGG rating: not found.
  URLs: https://frontlinegaming.org/2024/11/06/board-game-review-brass-birmingham/ ; https://www.fuzzyllamareviews.com/post/brass-birmingham ; https://boardgamestories.com/bgs-reviews/brass-birmingham-lancashire-review-which-one-i-prefer/ ; https://steamcommunity.com/app/1287920/discussions/0/3047231300186949614

- **Root.** Praised for deeply different factions and replay value; the clear cost is a steep learning curve because you must understand every faction to oppose it. Balance and runaway concerns come up (the Marquise runaway case), with the view that players are meant to balance the leader through negotiation and pressure. Strategists respond well to it if the group can commit multiple sessions. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://hexagamers.com/root-review/ ; https://punchboard.co.uk/root-board-game-review/ ; https://shopsavvy.com/reviews-tldr/root-board-game ; https://community.frontrowcrew.com/t/geeknights-tuesday-root/2047?page=2

- **7 Wonders.** Praised for fast simultaneous drafting with several paths to victory and good scaling to many players. Complaints are that it is multiplayer solitaire in practice, that you cannot plan far ahead because of what gets passed, that military is a love/hate element, and that scoring types overwhelm new players. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://hexagamers.com/7-wonders-review/ ; https://frontlinegaming.org/2024/05/15/board-game-review-7-wonders/ ; https://statelyplay.com/2017/11/14/review-7-wonders/ ; https://cultofthenew.com/2011/04/05/7-wonders/

- **Splendor.** Treated as a quintessential gateway engine builder that teaches in minutes and still holds strategy fans. Complaints from experienced players: little direct interaction, solitaire feel at low counts, a weak theme, and a sense of "solving" optimal pacing over time. Rating: not found.
  URLs: https://frontlinegaming.org/2025/05/09/board-game-review-splendor/ ; https://rolltoreview.com/splendor-review/ ; https://zatu.com/blogs/reviews/splendor-gamers-saying

## Gaps

- No page was opened in full. Every claim rests on search summaries, which can flatten nuance, and a few summaries read like generic text. Treat quoted-style judgments as indicative only.
- No BGG ratings, rank data, review counts or forum threads were reachable (BoardGameGeek, Reddit and Meeple Mountain are blocked; Dice Tower appeared only in a search listing).
- Brass: Birmingham evidence is thin; the first query was polluted by brass-band results and the useful pages were not readable.
- Raw heavy-gamer voices (BGG forums, Reddit) are underrepresented; the sources skew to review sites and blogs, which are more polite and more mixed-audience than a true heavy-gamer community.
- Theme counts are rough: "often / sometimes" reflects how many distinct pages surfaced in my searches, not a measured frequency across the hobby.
- Theme 13 (two-player and solo demand) and theme 11 (theme integration) rest on weaker, more generic sources.
- Seen only through a Strategist lens; no direct evidence about casual or family reactions beyond what reviewers mention in passing.
