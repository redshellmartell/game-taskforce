# Evidence: The Competitor

Last refreshed: 2026-10-03

Evidence basis: About 30 WebSearch queries, all read as search-result summaries only. Every WebFetch attempt (mechanicsofmagic.com, boardgamequest.com x2) was blocked by the egress proxy, so no page was opened in full. BoardGameGeek, Reddit, Wikipedia, Meeple Mountain and Dice Tower were unreachable by fetch. A few Reddit and Meeple Mountain URLs appeared in search listings, and I cite them only as listings. Counts of sources below are the number of distinct results whose summaries mentioned the point, not the number of reviews read. Many results are retailer review pages (zatu.com, hexagamers.com) and small blogs, which lean casual. Genuine tournament-community voices were thin.

## Recurring themes

1. First-player advantage and turn-order fairness matter a lot to this group. Complaint (when unaddressed). Frequency: often (about 6 sources). Competitive players on a digital-platform forum describe first seat as a big edge and call the compensation for later seats too small. A 2-player dataset for one big euro put first seat at roughly 51%. Another thread suggests that above about 55% in high-ranked online play, a designer should act. Splendor's first-seat edge is called slight and shrinks with more players.
   URLs: https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=248980 ; https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=147210 ; https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=108224 ; https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=122357 ; https://opinionatedgamers.com/2014/05/06/dale-yu-review-of-splendor/?nb=1

2. A game with a single dominant or "solved" line of play loses its appeal. Complaint. Frequency: often (about 4 sources). Once the best build order is known, the winner is on autopilot. Players value several viable routes, and this feeds replayability talk. Wingspan communities have flagged egg-spam and tucking as too strong, and the publisher has used redesigned mats and expansions to nerf them.
   URLs: https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2021/05/24/critical-play-dominion-or-dominance/ ; https://therewillbe.games/articles-essays/8942-variable-replayability ; https://draughtslondon.com/master-the-world-of-wingspan/ ; https://www.meeplemountain.com/top-six/top-6-strategies-for-flying-high-in-wingspan/

3. Multiple balanced paths to victory are praised. Praise. Frequency: often (about 5 sources). 7 Wonders is credited with strategies that check each other (all-in science invites a military neighbour). Splendor strategy guides describe engine and reservation lines as comparable. Wingspan is credited with several workable routes.
   URLs: https://www.boardgamequest.com/7-wonders-board-game-review/ ; https://islaythedragon.com/game-reviews/shelf-wear-1-a-seasoned-look-at-7-wonders/ ; https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Tips_splendor ; https://boardgamequest.com/wingspan-review

4. Luck that overrides skill is the classic complaint. Complaint. Frequency: often (about 6 sources). Pure-chance push-your-luck is seen as lacking strategy, while a "healthy mix" is accepted. Flip 7 draws the sharpest version: some reviewers say it is nearly all luck with only light card-counting, while others find the stop/push choice and watching rivals gives it more than expected. Port Royal reviewers say you cannot tell whether a win was skill or luck. Sea Salt & Paper is praised for keeping luck mitigable through discard-pile reading.
   URLs: https://hexagamers.com/flip-7-review/ ; https://boardgamequest.com/flip-7-review ; https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260110-flip-7-review ; https://boardgamequest.com/port-royal-review ; https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2015/5/1/the-worst-board-games ; https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02686v1

5. Runaway leaders and kingmaking are disliked. Complaint. Frequency: often (about 5 sources). Positive feedback loops that lock in a leader are criticised, and kingmaking is described as the thing that draws the most anger. Root is praised in the other direction: games stay close and early leaders rarely hold on.
   URLs: https://www.universityxp.com/news/2023/11/28/the-runaway-leader-problem ; https://www.skeletoncodemachine.com/p/kingmaking ; https://brandonthegamedev.com/?p=360 ; https://hexagamers.com/root-review/

6. Win-rate data and statistical balance get quoted. Praise for balance, complaint when skewed. Frequency: sometimes (about 4 sources). Communities use a rough 45-55% band as acceptable, and caution that equal win rates can hide very different experiences. For Root, one community-cited 4-player range ran from 17% to 31% by faction (25% would be even), but that comes from a Reddit listing I could not open, so treat it as unverified.
   URLs: https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/rootgame/comments/1jrnqmg/perfect_game_balance ; https://woehammer.com/2026/01/22/woehammer-data-literacy-same-win-rate-different-stories/ ; https://www.warhammer-community.com/2022/09/29/metawatch-how-the-warhammer-age-of-sigmar-team-uses-tournament-data-to-balance-the-game/

7. Analysis paralysis and slow play irritate others at the table. Complaint. Frequency: often (about 5 sources). Optimisers are described as the cause, with simultaneous action selection offered as a design cure. Azul gets this label, though more at 2 players where planning is deepest.
   URLs: https://brandonthegamedev.com/where-does-analysis-paralysis-come-from/ ; https://www.skeletoncodemachine.com/p/analysis-paralysis ; https://frontlinegaming.org/2019/08/24/gw-grognard-analysis-paralysis/ ; https://universityxp.com/news/2022/7/30/a-look-at-analysis-paralysis-in-board-games ; https://hexagamers.com/azul-review/

8. Endings should be tight and not drag. Complaint about long endgames, praise for hidden or variable end triggers. Frequency: sometimes (about 4 sources). Fixed-round finales are said to bloat as everyone optimises every point. Hidden scores and unpredictable end triggers are suggested. Sea Salt & Paper's Stop vs Last Chance call is praised as a tense end-of-round gamble.
   URLs: https://tabletopgamesblog.com/2025/09/23/to-the-bitter-end-board-games-dont-have-to-finish-topic-discussion ; https://therewillbe.games/articles-essays/8959-the-final-round ; https://whatsericplaying.com/2023/09/18/sea-salt-paper/ ; https://therewillbe.games/articles-boardgame-reviews/9424-push-it-real-good-your-luck-a-sea-salt-and-paper-board-game-review

9. "Multiplayer solitaire" and weak interaction draw criticism, and sharp interaction draws praise. Complaint / praise. Frequency: often (about 5 sources). Wingspan's limited interaction is the standard example. 7 Wonders restricts interaction to neighbours, and losing to someone you could not touch is annoying. Azul at 2 players is described as cutthroat, and at 3-4 as less controllable.
   URLs: https://opinionatedgamers.com/2019/01/27/wingspan/ ; https://www.nerdly.co.uk/2020/02/27/wingspan-board-game-review/ ; https://www.miniaturemarket.com/7wondersreview.html ; https://zatu.com/en-us/blogs/reviews/7-wonders-review ; https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/reviews/azul-review

10. Player count changes the competitive quality of a game. Praise or complaint depending on count. Frequency: often (about 6 sources). Sea Salt & Paper is called excellent at 2, with 4 adding downtime. Root feels off at low counts. Azul gets tighter at 2. 7 Wonders is best with a full table. Splendor's balance concerns fade with more players. Port Royal is said to work well at 2.
    URLs: https://whatsericplaying.com/2023/09/18/sea-salt-paper/ ; https://hexagamers.com/sea-salt-and-paper-review/ ; https://hexagamers.com/root-review/ ; https://zatu.com/en-ie/blogs/reviews/port-royal-review ; https://www.miniaturemarket.com/7wondersreview.html ; https://piefed.jeena.net/post/299542

11. Hard, deductive, hidden-information play is respected, as long as it can be tracked. Praise. Frequency: sometimes (about 3 sources). Card counting is seen as the base of deduction. Flip 7 players say tracking is nearly impossible at speed, so the benefit is small.
    URLs: https://onda.me/en/blog/essay-dogribgeim-esei---gongganeul-caeuneun-yesul-geim-07 ; https://whatsericplaying.com/2024/08/05/deductio/ ; https://hexagamers.com/flip-7-review/

12. Clear, precise rules and errata matter. Complaint when absent. Frequency: sometimes (about 4 sources). Competitive-minded sources call tight rulebooks a basis for fair play and note that ambiguity ruins flow. Brass: Birmingham reviews mention some unclear instructions. Root's rulebook and faction sheets get praise for teaching, though the learning curve is steep.
    URLs: https://level99store.com/blogs/news/board-game-bugs ; https://goonhammer.com/editorial-where-did-all-the-faqs-go ; https://www.shopsavvy.com/reviews-tldr/brass-birmingham-board-game ; https://gideonsgaming.com/root-board-game-review/

13. Catch-up mechanics divide opinion. Complaint and defence. Frequency: rarely (about 3 sources, mostly video-game context). The worry is that rubber-banding makes good play pointless. Defenders say it should create new decisions and preserve some reward for a lead.
    URLs: https://lawofgamedesign.com/2014/08/25/theory-rubber-bands/ ; https://destructoid.com/?p=217664

## Reactions to well-known games

Flip 7: Split. Casual-leaning reviewers call it a great quick filler. Strategy-minded ones call it nearly all luck with light probability and card-counting. Watching others' collections helps timing, but strategies do not always work. A reviewer note says four-player speed makes tracking cards impractical. Nominated for a 2025 German Game of the Year award. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://hexagamers.com/flip-7-review/ ; https://boardgamequest.com/flip-7-review ; https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260110-flip-7-review ; https://www.stackup.org/post/tabletop-review-flip-7 ; https://boardgamereview.co.uk/game-reviews/flip-7-card-game-review/

Port Royal: Reviewers like the draw-until-you-bust tension and the point that other players buy leftovers after you stop. Luck is a major factor, and early key cards matter. Bold play often pays off, "except when they don't". Good at 2 players. One reviewer suggests removing the Jesters as near worthless, a hint of a weak card. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://boardgamequest.com/port-royal-review ; https://miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/port-royal-review ; https://zatu.com/en-ie/blogs/reviews/port-royal-review ; https://www.meeplelikeus.co.uk/port-royal-2014/

Sea Salt & Paper: Praised for strategy hidden in a small deck. Reading discard piles and opponents rewards attention. Stop vs Last Chance is called agonising. Best at 2-3, and 4 adds downtime. One reviewer reports about 160 plays at 2 players. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://whatsericplaying.com/2023/09/18/sea-salt-paper/ ; https://hexagamers.com/sea-salt-and-paper-review/ ; https://www.boardgamequest.com/sea-salt-and-paper-review/ ; https://thefandomentals.com/sea-salt-and-paper-review ; https://therewillbe.games/articles-boardgame-reviews/9424-push-it-real-good-your-luck-a-sea-salt-and-paper-board-game-review

Wingspan: Engine-building and optimisation are enjoyed, but "multiplayer solitaire" is the common critique. Community concern over strong strategies (egg-spam, tucking, crows), and the publisher responded with new mats and expansions. One tucking engine in the Wetlands can reportedly yield 5-7 points per action. Metacritic score 84 (PC listing). BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://opinionatedgamers.com/2019/01/27/wingspan/ ; https://boardgamequest.com/wingspan-review ; https://www.nerdly.co.uk/2020/02/27/wingspan-board-game-review/ ; https://draughtslondon.com/master-the-world-of-wingspan/ ; https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/pc/wingspan

Pandemic: Quarterbacking (an alpha player directing everyone) is the headline complaint for co-op. A mathematically inclined player can come close to "solving" it. Others say the pull of the game is why people care. Difficulty is adjustable with extra epidemic cards, and wins are often razor-thin. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://gideonsgaming.com/board-game-quarterbacking-player-problem-or-game-problem/ ; https://gamesbeat.com/avidly-reads-board-games-excerpt-pandemic-mobile-saved-pandemic-from-me/ ; https://www.miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/pandemic-review ; https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=6845

Azul: Seen as sharp and tense. Each pick is a trade-off between your needs and what you hand to opponents. Luck of factory draws can strand you. At 2 players it is chess-like and cutthroat. At 3-4 it is more opportunistic and less plannable. Azul Duel (2025) is aimed at experienced 2-player fans. Some reviewers cite analysis paralysis. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/reviews/azul-review ; https://hexagamers.com/azul-review/ ; https://www.d20radio.com/main/review-azul/ ; https://piefed.jeena.net/post/299542 ; https://boardgameshop.nl/en/product/azul-duel/

Brass: Birmingham: Strongly praised for depth and replay value by experienced players. It reportedly knocked another title from the top BGG rank. Birmingham is said to forgive early mistakes more than Lancashire, which makes it harder to see what you did wrong. Common beginner errors are too many coal mines, ignoring low-level industries and dismissing beer. Complaints: intimidating rules, long setup, some unclear instructions, about 2 hours. BGG rank number: not found.
URLs: https://www.shopsavvy.com/reviews-tldr/brass-birmingham-board-game ; https://eriktwice.com/en/tag/brass/page/2/ ; https://punchboard.co.uk/brass-birmingham-review/ ; https://boardgamestories.com/bgs-reviews/brass-birmingham-lancashire-review-which-one-i-prefer/

Root: Praised for true asymmetry, close games and political shifts that stop early leaders running away. Balance is said to feel off at low player counts. Eyrie's programming can punish novices. Learning curve is steep because you must understand every faction. A community-cited win-rate range (17%-31% in 4-player) is from an unopened Reddit listing. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://hexagamers.com/root-review/ ; https://zatu.com/root-board-game-review/ ; https://gideonsgaming.com/root-board-game-review/ ; https://frontlinegaming.org/2023/12/23/board-game-review-root/ ; https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/rootgame/comments/1jrnqmg/perfect_game_balance

7 Wonders: Praised for multiple balanced paths, with the military and science checks. Interaction only with neighbours. Complicated end scoring hides who is winning, and losing to someone you could not interact with is a gripe. Best with a full table of seven. In the original, military can often be ignored, whereas the Duel version is tighter. A forum thread on first-player advantage exists for Duel. BGG rating: not found.
URLs: https://www.boardgamequest.com/7-wonders-board-game-review/ ; https://islaythedragon.com/game-reviews/rome-was-built-in-a-day-a-review-of-7-wonders/ ; https://www.miniaturemarket.com/7wondersreview.html ; https://zatu.com/en-us/blogs/reviews/7-wonders-review ; https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=108224

Splendor: Quick abstract engine builder. Slight first-player advantage that matters more when players want the same cards and less with more players. Multiple strategies (engine, reservation) are considered about equal. Guides stress spending gold carefully. BGG rating: not found.

## Gaps

- No page was opened in full. All findings come from search summaries, which can be lossy or generic.
- No BGG, Reddit or Dice Tower content was read directly, so there are no BGG ratings, rankings or forum threads on Brass, Root balance or Flip 7.
- Little from genuine tournament or league communities. Sources skew to retailer reviews and design blogs. Win-rate evidence is limited to a forum thread on one game and one unverified Reddit number.
- No hard data on a dominant strategy in Brass: Birmingham, Azul or Port Royal. The Brass search returned only general material.
- Flip 7 optimal-strategy search returned unrelated math papers, so there is no solid probability analysis on it.
- Several summaries were generic retailer text (for example Pandemic difficulty claims), so confidence in those is lower.
- Frequency counts are rough and based on search listings, not a structured survey.
