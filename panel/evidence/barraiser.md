# Evidence: The Bar Raiser (professional designer / expert critic)

Last refreshed: 2026-10-03

Evidence basis: THIN. 11 web searches were run; I read only the search-result summaries. Every attempt to open a page (opinionatedgamers.com, brandonthegamedev.com, meeplemountain.com, thethoughtfulgamer.com, stonemaiergames.com) was blocked by the network egress proxy, so no full article was read. BoardGameGeek and Reddit were not reached. Everything below is paraphrased from search summaries and is weaker than a normal evidence file. Several themes (dominant strategy, derivative design) rest on general design-advice pages, not on named professional designers. No ratings are cited except where a summary showed one.

## Recurring themes

1. Poorly written or unclear rules are a leading reason for rejection (complaint; often, 3 sources). Publishers want a finished, clearly written, attractive prototype. https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/board-game-design-dealing-rejection/ , https://brandonthegamedev.com/?p=2700 , https://pixune.com/blog/selling-your-board-game-ideas/
2. Fit and risk matter as much as quality: a good design can be turned down because of a publisher's schedule, budget or existing portfolio (neutral; often, 2 sources). https://brandonthegamedev.com/?p=2700 , https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/board-game-design-dealing-rejection/
3. Play time can end a pitch outright ("it takes too long") (complaint; sometimes, 1-2 sources). https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/board-game-design-dealing-rejection/
4. Rejection is normal even for strong designs, and experienced designers treat it as feedback to iterate on (neutral; sometimes, 2 sources). https://bumblingthroughdungeons.com/board-game-design-dealing-rejection/ , https://news.thegamecrafter.com/post/739421222044106752/dealing-with-rejection-the-dark-imp
5. Experts want one dominant strategy absent and several viable routes to a win; a scoring system that favours one path makes the rest pointless (complaint if absent; sometimes, 1 source seen, general design guidance). https://herotime1.com/academy/design/boardgame-card-game-playtesting-the-ultimate-blueprint-for-better-tabletop-game-design/
6. Originality is checked by asking whether the game is really a variant of an existing one (sometimes, 1 source). Same herotime1 URL.
7. Elegance is the highest praise: simple rules, deep or lively play, quick teach (praise; often, 4+ sources: Codenames, Ticket to Ride, Dixit, Catan). See game sections.
8. Fiddliness, bloat and slog draw expert complaints even for acclaimed games (complaint; often, 3 sources: Gloomhaven, Terraforming Mars, Twilight Imperium). See game sections.
9. Multiplayer solitaire / weak interaction is a recurring expert complaint of engine builders (complaint; sometimes, 1 source seen). https://boardgamequest.com/terraforming-mars-review
10. Heavy luck with little decision-making is dismissed as shallow, and a strong brand does not rescue it (complaint; sometimes, 2 sources). https://rolltoreview.com/?p=726 , https://hexagamers.com/exploding-kittens-review/
11. First-player advantage and early-mistake lock-in on long games are called out (complaint; rarely, 1 forum source). https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/boardgaming-in-2017/127711?page=55
12. Downtime matters: one action per turn keeps a game brisk and avoids analysis paralysis (praise; sometimes, 1 source). https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/?p=42755

## Reactions to well-known games

**Twilight Imperium (4th edition).** Reviewers describe it as too many parts that nonetheless works, and say the fourth edition trimmed awkward and unnecessary weight from the third. Role selection with a shared secondary ability keeps others engaged. The standing complaints are length (commonly 5-8 hours, sometimes far more) and downtime, and older forum posts on the series cite first-player and early-mistake problems. Sources: https://opinionatedgamers.com/2019/01/25/twilight-imperium-4th-edition/ , https://www.tabletopgaming.co.uk/?p=2061 , https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/boardgaming-in-2017/127711?page=55 (summaries only).

**Gloomhaven.** Widely acclaimed, with a BGG rating of about 8.8 cited in one summary. Experts note fiddliness, setup and rules overhead, and a heavy commitment; one reviewer found the battles turned into a slog late in the campaign. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloomhaven , https://opinionatedgamers.com/2020/11/28/gloomhaven-does-it-live-up-to-the-hype-review-by-rj-garrison/ , https://boardgamequest.com/quick-hits-gloomhaven-review

**Terraforming Mars.** Praised for card variety and corporation asymmetry, and for theme. Criticised for length with a slow end game, interaction that works like multiplayer solitaire, and component and rulebook weaknesses. Sources: https://boardgamequest.com/terraforming-mars-review , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terraforming_Mars_(board_game) (the "Why is it so popular" piece at thethoughtfulgamer.com was blocked).

**Catan.** Credited with starting the modern Eurogame wave and for a ten-minute teach with no rules lawyering. A minority view calls it fundamentally flawed, in the same family as Monopoly and Risk; another view says kingmaking is overstated. Sources: https://gamesradar.com/tabletop-gaming/catan-review , https://brandonthegamedev.com/5-lessons-from-catan-for-aspiring-board-game-designers/ , https://www.meeplemountain.com/news/settlers-of-catan-spiel-des-jahres-award-revoked-after-area-gamer-deems-game-broken/ (headline only).

**Codenames.** Strongest expert praise of the set: described as elegant, accessible and a "perfect ten" by one reviewer; won the 2016 Spiel des Jahres; Vlaada Chvatil is the designer. Small gripes: spymaster downtime and reliance on that role. Sources: https://opinionatedgamers.com/2015/08/20/dale-yu-review-of-codenames/ , https://www.geeksundergrace.com/tabletop/review-codenames/ , https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230126005367/en (SdJ context)

**Dixit.** Won the 2010 Spiel des Jahres; jury praised artwork and imagination; simple rules for all ages. I did not see critical expert analysis, so the reception beyond the award is thin. Sources: https://opinionatedgamers.com/2015/12/20/sdj-re-reviews-32-dixit/ (title only), https://godisageek.com/2013/11/dixit-review/

**Exploding Kittens.** Funded with about $8.7M on Kickstarter. Critics call it luck-heavy and shallow, with one calling it a triumph of branding over accomplishment. Defenders see a fast, family-friendly party filler. Sources: https://rolltoreview.com/?p=726 , https://hexagamers.com/exploding-kittens-review/ , https://icv2.com/print/article/17632

**Ticket to Ride.** Seen as a model gateway design: one action per turn, brisk, learnable in about 15 minutes, with little downtime. Sources: https://techraptor.net/tabletop/reviews/ticket-to-ride-review , https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/?p=42755
