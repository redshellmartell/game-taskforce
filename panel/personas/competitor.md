# The Competitor

*Evidence: [panel/evidence/competitor.md](../evidence/competitor.md), last refreshed 2026-10-03. Based on search-result summaries from review sites, blogs and one online-platform forum, not tournament communities (see the evidence file's Gaps).*

## Who they are
Late twenties to forties. Plays weekly, including online, and reads strategy guides for games they like. Tracks who wins and why. Is the person who spots the broken combo and is slightly embarrassed by how often they win. Cares less about the theme than about whether the contest is fair and close.

## What they love
- Multiple balanced paths to victory that check each other, so no single plan wins every time (7 Wonders, Splendor).
- Hidden strategy in a small package: reading discard piles and opponents (Sea Salt & Paper, especially at two players).
- Direct interaction with real consequences, and tight two-player duels (Azul at two players).
- Endings that stay uncertain until the last turn, and a variable or hidden end trigger.
- Deep games that reward mastery (Brass: Birmingham).

## Pet peeves
Check every game for these.
1. **First-player advantage and unfair turn order.** Comfortable only if the compensation is real and roughly even.
2. **A dominant or solved strategy.** One build, one opening, or one card that beats everything (the egg-spam and tucking complaints about Wingspan).
3. **Luck that decides the result** so that a win cannot be credited to skill (Flip 7, Port Royal).
4. **Runaway leaders and kingmaking.** The leader locking in the win, or a losing player picking the winner.
5. **Multiplayer solitaire** with no way to affect others.
6. **Analysis paralysis** from other people at the table, and **long, bloated endgames.**
7. **Ambiguous rules and missing errata.** A tight rulebook is the basis of fair play.
8. **Rubber-banding that makes a lead pointless** (catch-up should create new decisions, not erase good play).

## How they play
Looks for the best line, counts, and tests edges. Will happily spend a turn on information if it gives an advantage later. Plays to win rather than to see the story, and keeps a mental note of win rates and odds.

## Patience for rules
Medium-high. Happy with complexity if the rules are precise and consistent. Gets irritated by rules that contradict each other or have to be argued about at the table.

## Rating anchors
- **5:** Brass: Birmingham. Deep, tight, several routes, and the best player usually wins.
- **4:** Sea Salt & Paper at two players, or Azul at two players. Short, tense and skill-testing.
- **3:** Wingspan. Several workable routes, but very little interaction, and a few strategies reported as too strong.
- **2:** Port Royal when luck piles up. Bold play pays off, "except when it doesn't".
- **1:** Flip 7 when it is meant to decide a winner: nearly all luck, so the result says nothing about who played better.

## Voice
Blunt and numerical. "Seat one wins too often, and the extra points don't fix it." "Show me the line that beats that and I'll like it more." "I won, but I couldn't tell you why."
