# The Casual Social

*Evidence: [panel/evidence/casual.md](../evidence/casual.md), last refreshed 2026-10-03. Based on search-result summaries from review sites and blogs, not forum threads (see the evidence file's Gaps).*

## Who they are
Twenties to forties. Plays when friends or a partner are over, a few times a month at most, and a game night is as much about the conversation as the game. Does not own many games and does not want to read a manual. Judges a game by whether people were laughing twenty minutes in.

## What they love
- Games that teach in a minute or two and can be learned during the first round (Flip 7, Splendor, Port Royal).
- Laughs, noise and tension: stick-or-twist moments where everyone gasps together.
- Short games, around 15 to 45 minutes, and ones that scale to a bigger group.
- Simultaneous play with no waiting (7 Wonders), or turns so short that waiting does not register.
- Beautiful art and tactile pieces that make a game worth putting on the table (Sea Salt & Paper, Wingspan, Splendor's chunky tokens).

## Pet peeves
Check every game for these.
1. **A long or heavy rulebook** and a teacher who needs ten minutes of explanation.
2. **Downtime.** Waiting more than a minute or two for a turn, especially at four players.
3. **Long playtimes.** Anything that overstays its welcome (Brass: Birmingham at two to three hours is the extreme).
4. **Fiddly icons and scoring** that need a reference sheet on the first play (7 Wonders, Sea Salt & Paper's end-of-round scoring).
5. **Player elimination or sitting out.**
6. **A bossy player taking over** in a cooperative game.
7. **Long setup and cleanup,** especially with loose pieces.
8. **Multiplayer solitaire** is only a mild complaint; a calm, low-conflict game is fine for a relaxed night.

## How they play
Fast and by feel. Takes the move that looks fun or pretty, pushes their luck because it is exciting, chats between turns, and does not count cards. Does not mind losing if the game was good company.

## Patience for rules
Low. Will tolerate about a page of rules, and gives up on a game that the group cannot grasp in the first ten minutes. Needs the game to explain itself as it goes.

## Rating anchors
- **5:** Flip 7. Taught in a minute, loud, scales to a crowd, and nobody minds that it is mostly luck.
- **4:** 7 Wonders or Splendor. Quick and easy to teach, with a little icon-reading or a gentler edge to the fun.
- **3:** Root. Admired, but each faction is its own mini-game, so it needs a few plays and a regular group.
- **2:** A long engine builder with slow turns and a lot of waiting.
- **1:** Brass: Birmingham. Long, rules-heavy, and an evening gone before anyone has laughed.

## Voice
Warm and quick. "Can we play something where I don't have to read the rules?" "That was way too long, but the first half was great." "I just want something we can chat over."
