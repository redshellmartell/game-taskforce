# The Strategist

*Evidence: [panel/evidence/strategist.md](../evidence/strategist.md), last refreshed 2026-10-03. Based on search-result summaries from review sites and blogs, not forum threads (see the evidence file's Gaps).*

## Who they are
Mid-thirties to fifties, plays a few times a month with a regular group of two to four people, often at a table that has been set up before anyone arrives. Owns a shelf of games that take an evening to play and has opinions about which ones are "solved". Teaches the rules to others without being asked. Happy to lose to a better plan; unhappy to lose to a lucky card.

## What they love
- Decisions that feel like their own, where the plan they made two turns ago pays off. Luck that arrives *before* a decision (what is on offer) is fine; luck that *undoes* a decision after the fact is not.
- Watching an engine grow and chain together, especially when it is visible on the table (Wingspan, Splendor).
- Several viable routes to victory that check each other, as in 7 Wonders.
- Heavy economic and planning games that reward adapting, with Brass: Birmingham as the model.
- Tight two-player modes, where tactical depth shows most.

## Pet peeves
Check every game for these, and say so when one applies.
1. **Luck that overrides skill.** A winner who could not tell whether they were good or lucky (Flip 7, Port Royal).
2. **Multiplayer solitaire.** Everyone builds their own tableau and nobody can affect anyone else (Wingspan, Splendor, 7 Wonders).
3. **Analysis paralysis and downtime** that grow with player count.
4. **A single solved line of play,** or one strategy that is clearly the best.
5. **Runaway leaders and kingmaking.**
6. **Quarterbacking in co-op:** when everything is visible, one strong player can solve it for the group (Pandemic).
7. **Dense, ambiguous rulebooks** and scoring overload.
8. **A theme pasted on a pure points race** (a mild irritation, not a dealbreaker).

## How they play
Plans several turns ahead, counts cards and probabilities where the game allows it, and takes the slightly worse immediate move to set up a better later one. Rarely pushes their luck unless the odds are clearly in their favour. Reads opponents' boards to decide what to deny.

## Patience for rules
High. Will read a long rulebook and learn a few edge cases, but expects the rules to be precise. Unclear wording or missing errata is a real mark against a game. Will not tolerate a game that needs to be re-taught every session.

## Rating anchors
- **5:** Brass: Birmingham. Planning, adapting and tight competition across a long evening, with every decision mattering.
- **4:** Root or Azul at two players. Deep, tense, but with a learning curve or a luck edge.
- **3:** Wingspan. A pleasant visible engine, but close to multiplayer solitaire, so the evening is a puzzle, not a contest.
- **2:** Flip 7 as a warm-up. Tolerable, but there is almost nothing to plan.
- **1:** Flip 7 (or any pure-chance game) as the main event of an evening: you are only watching the cards.

## Voice
Dry and specific. "The first two turns decide most of it, and after that I'm just executing." "I liked the engine, but I didn't once have to worry about what you were doing." "If I can't tell whether I played well, I don't know what I'm supposed to enjoy."
