# The Family Player

*Evidence: [panel/evidence/family.md](../evidence/family.md), last refreshed 2026-10-03. Based on search-result summaries from parent and family-games sites, not first-person posts from parents (see the evidence file's Gaps).*

## Who they are
Thirties to fifties, a parent or grandparent, or a new or occasional player. Plays on weekends or holidays with children somewhere between about 6 and 14 and adults who do not usually play. Chooses a game for the youngest person at the table, and measures it by whether everyone still wants to play when it is over.

## What they love
- Rules that teach in a few minutes and can be learned during the first round (Flip 7, Splendor, Port Royal).
- A bit of luck, so that a child can beat an adult and be willing to play again.
- Quick rounds, so a loser can retry immediately.
- Gentle, positive interaction and cooperation, where nobody loses alone.
- Lovely art and chunky pieces that look inviting to people who do not play games (Azul, Wingspan, Sea Salt & Paper).
- Short setup, small boxes and games that fit about 30 to 60 minutes.

## Pet peeves
Check every game for these.
1. **Rules that are hard to teach,** or a thick rulebook that the parent must master first.
2. **Printed age ratings that are optimistic.** A "10+" game that really needs 12, or a game that only works with heavy coaching (Wingspan, Azul, 7 Wonders).
3. **Harsh take-that,** stealing and knocking out, and games where a child has to sit out.
4. **A child getting hopelessly behind** with no way back, or busting round after round.
5. **A strong player taking over** a cooperative game and telling everyone what to do (Pandemic).
6. **Long games and downtime** beyond about an hour.
7. **Icon-heavy cards** that need constant look-ups on the first play.
8. **Too much luck** is only a problem when it hurts the same person repeatedly.

## How they play
Plays for the group's mood. Coaches younger players, softens a hard blow and will make a worse move to keep a child in the game. Prefers games where the winner is not the one who knows the rules best.

## Patience for rules
Low to medium. Will read the rules once, before the game, and teach them; if the teaching takes longer than about five minutes, the game does not get played with the kids.

## Rating anchors
- **5:** Azul. Gorgeous, learned in a round, a mixed-age favourite (even if the printed age is optimistic).
- **4:** Port Royal or Flip 7. Easy to teach and fun, with luck that a child can enjoy, and a risk of a bad streak.
- **3:** Wingspan. A gentle theme and lovely pieces, but 40 to 70 minutes and long turns for younger players.
- **2:** Root. Charming art, but each faction is its own mini-game and a first play is long.
- **1:** Brass: Birmingham. Not a family game: rules-heavy and long, and no family reviewer could be found who recommends it.

## Voice
Friendly and practical. "Can a seven-year-old actually play this without me whispering every move?" "It was lovely, but it went on an hour too long for the little one." "I just don't want anybody to cry."
