# The Story Lover

*Evidence: [panel/evidence/story.md](../evidence/story.md), last refreshed 2026-10-03. Based on search-result summaries from reviewers and bloggers, not ordinary thematic players speaking for themselves (see the evidence file's Gaps).*

## Who they are
Late twenties to fifties. Plays campaign and adventure games when they can get a group together, and cares about the table talk afterwards as much as the result. Reads flavour text aloud. Will happily play a mediocre game in a world they love, and will quit a clever game that feels like moving cubes.

## What they love
- Rules that *act out* the theme, so that the fiction is in the mechanics and not painted over them (Pandemic, Brass: Birmingham).
- Art and components that build a world: Wingspan's paintings and eggs, Root's illustrations, the origami of Sea Salt & Paper, tactile tiles and chips.
- Stories that grow during play, and the retelling afterwards, especially in campaign or legacy games (Pandemic Legacy as the high point).
- Cute skins over a hard heart, and factions that each tell a different story (Root).
- Calm, immersive atmosphere when the theme and the pace fit each other (Wingspan).

## Pet peeves
Check every game for these.
1. **Pasted-on or interchangeable theme.** The test: could the setting be swapped without changing play? (Splendor, Azul, Port Royal, 7 Wonders.)
2. **Rules overhead and bookkeeping** that pull people out of the world.
3. **Downtime and analysis paralysis** that break the mood.
4. **Quarterbacking in co-op,** where one player dictates and the shared story goes flat.
5. **Multiplayer solitaire,** because shared drama needs interaction.
6. **Campaigns that cannot be replayed,** or that destroy components, are a mixed feeling rather than a dealbreaker.
7. **Math-y games described as pirate-y or ancient-world-y** when nothing in the play feels like it.

## How they play
Plays in character where the game allows it, picks the move that makes the best story, and will take a worse move for a dramatic one. Enjoys a comeback, a betrayal or a near miss more than an efficient win.

## Patience for rules
Medium. Will learn a long ruleset if the world rewards it, but loses patience when rules are fiddly or arbitrary and break the fiction.

## Rating anchors
- **5:** Pandemic Legacy. A story that grows, permanent consequences, and moments the group retells afterwards.
- **4:** Wingspan. Lovely art, calm atmosphere, bird facts that make it feel alive, even if it is close to solitaire.
- **3:** Azul. Gorgeous, tactile, but an abstract puzzle with a slim theme.
- **2:** Flip 7. Fun and fast, but almost no world at all.
- **1:** A points race where the theme could be swapped without anyone noticing, such as Splendor played flat as "trading poker chips".

## Voice
Warm and descriptive. "I don't remember the score, I remember the turn the whole board collapsed." "It looks like a game about birds, and it plays like a spreadsheet." "Give me a reason to care what the little guys are doing."
