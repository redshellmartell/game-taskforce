# Owner idea, researched

Working title: **Archetypes** | slug: `archetypes` | idea-bank id: `archetypes-questionnaire-rpg` | source: owner

## What the owner fixed (respected as given)
- Role-playing game.
- Character creation is a personality questionnaire; answers produce the result (no stat blocks, no class list).
- The result offers several avatars; the player picks one.
- The point is roleplaying an ARCHETYPE (a way of thinking and acting), not a named character with a backstory.
- Inspired by a 16-type model (four two-sided traits, 16 combinations).
- IP: original questionnaire, trait names and archetype names. Nothing from 16Personalities or Myers-Briggs (questions, type names, descriptions, four-letter codes, art).

## Open questions: PROPOSALS for the owner to decide
None of these were given by the owner. Defaults are my suggestion only.

1. **Format.** (a) Card deck plus a one-page questionnaire sheet (RECOMMENDED: studio's usual output, cheap to prototype). (b) Booklet RPG. (c) Phone/web quiz plus printed cards.
2. **Players and time.** (a) 3-5 players, about 45 minutes, one-shot (RECOMMENDED). (b) 2-4, 90 minutes. (c) Open-ended, 3-6, campaign.
3. **GM or GM-less.** (a) GM-less, scenes rotate and the table frames them (RECOMMENDED: fits non-RPG groups; Fiasco-style). (b) One rotating GM. (c) Fixed GM.
4. **Avatars offered.** (a) Three: best match, a near neighbour, and a "stretch" opposite (RECOMMENDED). (b) Top two only. (c) Top five.
5. **What the archetype changes in play.** (a) Each archetype card gives a drive, a signature move and a blind spot; you earn a token for acting in type and spend it for a boost (RECOMMENDED). (b) Scene goals only. (c) Purely narrative, no mechanics.
6. **Audience.** (a) Friends/story-curious players and party groups (RECOMMENDED). (b) Classrooms and teams as an icebreaker. (c) Experienced RPG players.

Proposed numbers (all proposals): 3-5 players, 45 min, complexity 1.5.

## Comparables
- **Ultima IV** (1985): a fortune-teller asks moral dilemmas and the top-ranked virtue sets your class and start city. Proof that questions-as-creation works; it is a video game and the result is one forced class.
- **Fiasco / playbook GM-less games** (Powered by the Apocalypse style): archetypal playbooks make creation easy for newcomers and GM-less play suits non-gamers. They start from a menu pick, not a questionnaire.
- **Character-questionnaire tools in TTRPGs:** common as prompts, but they serve a named character's backstory, the opposite of this idea.

## Market gap and evidence
- Gap (modest, not proven): I found no tabletop game where a quiz produces your role and the goal is to play a type of person. Search results showed personality-flavoured party and deduction games (Emotional Intelligence on Kickstarter) and quiz-to-class web tools, not this. Absence in search is weak evidence.
- Demand for the framing: the 16-type model is hugely popular culturally even though academics dispute it (about half of takers get a different type after five weeks, per critics). Honest implication: market it as a mirror and play prompt, never as an assessment.
- Risk: some players dislike personality-test framing; a "stretch" avatar option helps.

## Honest note on testing
This is a role-playing game. The studio's simulation playtest suits it less than card games. The playtester CAN test: questionnaire logic (all 16 outcomes reachable, balanced distribution, no dead types), avatar-offer rules, token economy and scene-flow length via simulation. It CANNOT test: whether roleplaying feels fun, whether the questions feel fair, tone, or table dynamics. Those need human sessions; the owner's playtests are the real check. Simulatability is scored low for this reason.

## Rubric (max 30)
Demand 3, Gap 4, Originality 4, Producibility 4, Simulatability 2, Owner fit 4 = **21/30**.

## Constraints for the designer
- Original trait names, questions, archetype names; no resemblance to MBTI codes or type labels.
- Four original two-sided traits giving 16 archetypes; questionnaire at most 12 questions, 5 minutes.
- Components: about 16 archetype cards plus one question sheet and a few tokens; printable at home.
- Do not decide the open questions above without the owner's answers; build around the recommended defaults only if the owner accepts them.
