# Brief: Nine Lives Dungeon

- **Working title / slug:** Nine Lives Dungeon / `solo-nine-card-roguelike`
- **Idea-bank id:** `solo-nine-card-roguelike` (scan of 2026-10-03; brief written 2026-10-04 from the bank, no new searches)

## Target
- **Players:** 1 (solo only)
- **Play time:** 10 minutes per run (cap 12); a full "nine lives" session of several runs is about 30-40 minutes
- **Audience:** solo players, commuters and travellers; adults and teens (12+)
- **Complexity:** 2 / 5

## Core mechanics and theme
- A roguelike in **nine cards in a tuckbox**. Each run, the same nine cards are shuffled and laid out as a dungeon (a 3x3 grid or a path; the designer decides). The nine cards are never added to or removed, only rearranged and flipped, so the game is a permutation puzzle with push-your-luck.
- Death carries over: a cat adventurer has nine lives, and each death changes the next run (for example one card flips to its other face or gains a permanent mark). This is the roguelike "meta" layer. Keep it to a few marks that fit on the cards or a pencil tally.
- Theme direction: a cat adventurer with nine lives. Tone is light and cosy-dark.
- Success means a run feels different each time and a loss teaches something, rather than just ending.

## Market gap
- Solo play is a top-3 component of 2020-2025 releases, and small portable boxes are praised (Opinionated Gamers, zatu 2026 solo-boom piece).
- Reddit-based summaries rate small solo card games highly (Friday 4.5, Onirim 4.5), so demand is real.
- Gap is only 3: the solo card space is crowded. Nearest comparables grow or deplete a deck; a fixed nine-card permutation roguelike with persistent death effects is the proposed difference. The twist must carry the design.

## Constraints for the designer
- Components: **nine cards** (at most 12 including a rules card), one tuckbox. No dice. Tokens only as optional pencil tallies.
- Rules fit one page, with at most 3 special rules. Set-up under 30 seconds.
- Each card must matter in every run: zero dead cards, and no single layout that always wins (no dominant opening).
- Needs a clear win condition for a run, a clear loss, and a score ladder across a nine-lives session.
- Persistence must stay simple: no sealed content, no legacy-style changes (see the banked Eight Nights idea, rejected for that reason).
- KPIs: bot win rate in a target band for a competent player (suggested 35-65%) and strategic-vs-random win gap 20 points or more; simulated run length within 20% of 10 minutes (8-12); no rule ambiguities.
- Seat balance and lead changes do not apply (solo); replace them with variety: at least several distinct winning layouts and run-to-run outcome spread.

## What a solo bot simulation can and cannot test
- **Can test:** rules coverage and ambiguities, win and loss rates, run length in turns, dead cards, dominant layouts (exhaustive search over permutations of nine cards is feasible: 9! = 362,880), how death carry-over shifts difficulty across a nine-lives session, strategic-vs-random gap, score ladder distribution.
- **Cannot test:** whether it feels fun or fresh after repeated runs, whether deaths feel fair or frustrating, flavour and theme, tactile handling, and whether the permutation puzzle is satisfying rather than just solvable. Only human solo playtests answer these. Panel scoring has no seat or interaction signals for a one-player game, so treat panel numbers as weak.
