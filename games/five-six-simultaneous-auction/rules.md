# Last Bid Standing - Rules (v3, revision 2)

## 1. Overview

- **Title:** Last Bid Standing
- **Hook:** An estate sale where everyone bids at once and every losing bid is burned. Burned bids become **Hype**: the money people waste on a kind of item sets what that kind of item is worth, up to a ceiling. But the hottest market is a bubble. At the end of the game the most-hyped category **crashes** and its Hype is worth nothing. Tied top bids knock each other out, so the last bid standing wins.
- **Players:** 5-6 (built for these counts; the same deck at both)
- **Play time:** about 20 minutes (14 rounds of about 65 seconds, plus 2 minutes of setup and scoring)
- **Age:** 10+
- **Complexity:** 2 / 5

## 2. Components

78 cards in total: 28 Lot cards, 44 Bid cards and 6 Paddle cards. Bid cards and Paddles have the **same card back** (see section 5). Lots have a different back. A pencil and paper are optional for scoring.

There are **four categories**, each with its own symbol: **Clocks**, **Silver**, **Paintings** and **Books**. Every Lot card and every Bid card shows one category.

**Lot cards (28): 7 per category.** Each shows a category and a printed **value** (2 to 5).

| Category | Value 2 | Value 2 | Value 3 | Value 3 | Value 4 | Value 4 | Value 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clocks | Alarm Clock | Cuckoo Clock | Mantel Clock | Carriage Clock | Pocket Watch | Wall Regulator | Grandfather Clock |
| Silver | Teaspoons | Napkin Rings | Candlesticks | Gravy Boat | Tea Service | Punch Bowl | Silver Samovar |
| Paintings | Postcard Sketch | Paint-by-Numbers | Seascape | Still Life | Portrait of Aunt May | Gilt Landscape | Unsigned Masterpiece |
| Books | Paperbacks | Cookbook | Atlas | Encyclopedia Set | Signed First Edition | Leather Folio | Lost Manuscript |

Per category the values total 23; the deck totals 92.

**Bid cards (44): 11 per category**, with bid values 1 to 11, each value once per category (so each bid value exists 4 times in the deck).

| Category | Bid values | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Clocks | 1-11 | 11 |
| Silver | 1-11 | 11 |
| Paintings | 1-11 | 11 |
| Books | 1-11 | 11 |

A Bid card's **value** decides who wins; its **category** decides which Hype row it joins if it loses.

**Paddle cards (6):** one per seat, numbered 1-6. The front says **PASS** and carries the rules summary (including the Hype ceiling of +4). A Paddle is never spent, burned or drawn.

## 3. Setup

1. Each player takes one Paddle. With 5 players, return Paddle 6 to the box.
2. Shuffle the 28 Lot cards and place them as a **face-up** pile, the **Lot deck**. Its top card is always visible (the "preview").
3. Shuffle the 44 Bid cards. Deal **4 face down to each player** as their hand. Place the rest face down as the **Bid deck**. Leave room for a face-up **discard pile** next to it.
4. Leave room for the **block** (the lots for sale) and for four **Hype rows**, one per category. All start empty.
5. There is no first player: every round is simultaneous. Paddle numbers are used only to order income draws (section 5), which gives no advantage because the deck is shuffled.

**Public information:** the number of cards in each hand, the discard pile, the Hype rows, the block, the Lot deck's top card and everyone's won lots. **Secret:** the cards in each hand (including all income draws), and the card each player places in phase 2 until the reveal.

## 4. Turn structure

The game lasts exactly **14 rounds**. Every round has these five phases, in order.

1. **Lots up.** Take the top 2 cards of the Lot deck and add them to the **block**. Lots left unsold from earlier rounds stay on the block, so the block can hold more than 2 lots. The new top card of the Lot deck is the preview for next round (in round 14 there is no preview).
2. **Bid.** Each player secretly chooses exactly one card and places it face down in front of them: either **one Bid card** from their hand, or their **Paddle** (a pass). A player with no Bid cards must play their Paddle. A placed card cannot be taken back or changed. When everyone has placed a card, go to phase 3.
3. **Reveal.** All players turn their card face up at the same time.
4. **Resolve (special rules 1 and 2).**
   1. **Cancel ties:** any bid value played by two or more players is **cancelled** for all of them (categories do not matter; only the value).
   2. **First bidder:** the player with the highest bid value still standing takes **any one** lot from the block into their collection (face up in front of them).
   3. **Second bidder:** the player with the next-highest bid value still standing takes **any one** of the lots still on the block.
   4. The first and second bidders' Bid cards go face up on the **discard pile**.
   5. **Burn:** every other Bid card played this round (lower bids and every cancelled bid) is **burned**: place it face up in the **Hype row of its own category**. Burned cards never return to play.
   6. Lots not taken **stay on the block** for the next round.
   7. Paddles return to their owners' hands.
5. **Income.** Each player who played their Paddle this round draws **2 Bid cards** from the Bid deck **face down** into their hand, following the income procedure in section 5. Income is drawn in **every** round, including round 14 (cards drawn in round 14 matter only for the tiebreakers in section 6).

After round 14, the game ends (section 6).

### Every legal action, summarised
| Action | When legal | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Bid | You hold at least 1 Bid card | Play one Bid card face down. If it is a standing first or second bid, you take a lot and the card is discarded; otherwise it is burned into the Hype row of its category |
| Pass | Always | Play your Paddle face down; draw 2 Bid cards face down in phase 5 |
| Choose a lot | You are the first or second bidder | First bidder takes any lot on the block; then second bidder takes any remaining lot |

There are no other actions. Players may not play more than one card, make binding deals, trade, show or give cards. Players may say anything; nothing said is binding.

**Example (6 players).** The block holds Pocket Watch (Clocks 4), carried over from last round, plus Gravy Boat (Silver 3) and Teaspoons (Silver 2), just dealt. Bids: 11 (Books), 11 (Clocks), 7 (Silver), 4 (Clocks), Pass, Pass. The two 11s cancel. 7 is the first bidder and takes Pocket Watch; 4 is the second bidder and takes Gravy Boat. Teaspoons stays on the block. The 7 and the 4 go to the discard pile. The Books 11 goes to the Books Hype row and the Clocks 11 to the Clocks Hype row. The two passers each draw 2 Bid cards face down.

## 5. Special rules and timing

The game has exactly three special rules:
1. **Last bid standing.** Tied bid values cancel each other; the highest **untied** bid is first, the next-highest untied bid is second.
2. **Burned bids become Hype, up to +4.** Every losing Bid card goes into the Hype row of the category printed on it. A category's **Hype** is the number of cards in its row; each lot of that category gains **at most +4** from Hype (section 6).
3. **The bubble.** At the end of the game, exactly one category crashes: the one with the most Hype (counting every card in the row, not capped; ties broken as in section 6). Its lots gain nothing from Hype.

Timing and edge cases:
- **Fewer than two bids standing:** if only one bid value is standing, that player takes one lot; nobody is second. If none is standing (everyone passed, or every bid was tied), nobody takes a lot and every played Bid card is burned. In both cases the untaken lots stay on the block.
- **Bid of any value can win.** A 1 that is the only standing bid takes a lot. There is no minimum bid and no reserve price.
- **Income procedure** (the only draw rule). Let P be the number of players who passed this round.
  1. If P = 0, skip income.
  2. If the Bid deck holds fewer than 2 x P cards, first shuffle the discard pile together with all cards left in the Bid deck to form a new Bid deck (simulation: uniform random shuffle). This is the only time the discard pile is shuffled. If the discard pile is empty, this step just shuffles the cards left in the deck.
  3. Each passer draws K = the smaller of 2 and (Bid deck size after step 2, divided by P, rounded down), dealt one card at a time in rising Paddle number. If K = 0, nobody draws this round.
  Example: 3 players pass, the Bid deck holds 4 cards and the discard pile is empty. Step 2 shuffles the 4 cards; K = the smaller of 2 and 1 = 1, so each passer draws 1 card and 1 card stays in the deck. Every passer always draws the same number, so Paddle order gives no advantage.
- **Why the Paddle shares a back.** Players place their cards one after another; matching backs mean a late placer cannot see who passed, so placing last gives no information.
- **No simultaneous conflicts:** all bids are chosen at the same time in phase 2; the only later choices are the first bidder's lot, then the second bidder's lot, in that order.
- **No hand limit.** Hand sizes are public.
- **Lots on the block after round 14** are out of the game and score for nobody.

## 6. End of game and scoring

**Trigger:** the game ends at the end of round 14, when the Lot deck is empty. The number of rounds never changes.

**Scoring.**
1. **Hype:** count the Bid cards in each category's Hype row. That number is the category's Hype.
2. **The bubble bursts:** the category with the highest Hype crashes. Exactly one category crashes. If two or more categories tie for the highest Hype, the crash goes to the tied category whose row has the **highest total of printed bid values** (1-11 on each card in that row). If still tied, the one holding the **highest single bid value**; if still tied, compare the next-highest card, and so on. If the rows are identical, the first in this order crashes: Clocks, Silver, Paintings, Books. If every row is empty, nothing crashes.
3. **Hype bonus:** the crashed category's bonus is **0**. Every other category's bonus is its Hype, **but never more than 4**.
4. **Each lot** in your collection scores its **printed value + its category's Hype bonus**.
5. Bid cards left in hand score nothing.

Example: final Hype is Clocks 7, Silver 7, Paintings 5, Books 3. Clocks and Silver tie; the Clocks row totals 41 in bid values and the Silver row 35, so Clocks crashes. Bonuses: Clocks 0, Silver 4, Paintings 4, Books 3. A player holding Grandfather Clock (5), Gravy Boat (3) and Seascape (3) scores (5 + 0) + (3 + 4) + (3 + 4) = 19.

**Winner:** highest score. If players tie, apply these among the tied players only, in order: **Tiebreaker 1:** most Bid cards left in hand. **Tiebreaker 2:** highest total value of Bid cards left in hand. **Still tied:** those players share the win.

## 7. Design notes

**The twist: sunk cost sets the price, and the hottest price crashes.** A losing bid becomes Hype for the category printed on the Bid card, not the lot being sold, so every card is two decisions: how much to bid, and which market you feed if you lose. The bubble stops snowballing: the best place is the *second*-hottest market.

**What v3 changes in best play.** The +4 ceiling splits each category's life in two. Below 4 Hype, burning into your own category adds value to your lots. At 4 or more, a further burn adds nothing to anyone's score and only pushes that category toward the crash. So once a category is "full", burning into it is purely an attack on whoever holds it, and protecting your own full category means burning *elsewhere*. This makes the category of each losing card a sharper choice than in v2, where every extra Hype was still worth points.

**Comeback (L3).** Passing pays 2 cards, so a player who falls behind builds a hand and buys the rich block that collisions leave behind. The leader's main category is the obvious target to push into the crash, and once it is at the ceiling every rival can attack it at no cost to their own score. Round 1 cannot decide the game: at most 2 lots (about 7 of 92 printed points) move, and Hype is set by the whole game. KPIs: runaway leader at most 65%, lead changes at least 2.

**Twist ablation (L1).** Each "ignore-it" bot must lose by at least 5 points of win rate against the full strategic bot at the same table, at **both** 5 and 6 players:
- *Ignore-Hype bot:* chooses bids by lot value only and ignores which category it burns into. (v2: -6.5 / +13.1, inconsistent; must be settled.)
- *Ignore-crash bot:* treats the top category as safe. (v2: +7.5 / +5.5, pass; must still pass.)
- *Ignore-cap bot (new target, no new rule):* values Hype as if it had no ceiling, so it keeps feeding categories already at 4+ as if that scored. If it does not lose by 5 points, the ceiling is only a score scaler, which is acceptable: its main job is item 1 of the targets below.
- *Ignore-ties bot:* never considers collisions (always bids its best card). The full bot must estimate collisions from **public counts only** (own hand, discard pile, Hype rows; everything else is unseen). The tie rule is kept because it is what creates carried-over lots and low-bid wins (unsold lots 2.9 and forced passes 5.1% depend on it), and cutting it would need a new tie-break rule, which is not simpler. If ignore-ties fails again at either count, the rule stays for pacing only and the design notes will stop claiming it as a skill lever.

**Target bands and knobs (L5, L8).** At 5 and 6 players: seat gap at most 5 points; **Hype share of points 45-50%, knob: the Hype ceiling (4 now; 3 if share is above 50%, 5 if below 45%)**; lot values stay 2-5; forced passes at most 13% and lots left on the block at most 5 (knob: starting hand 4 or 5); skill gap at least 20 points for a lone strategic bot and at least 5 at a 50/50 mixed table. Expected Hype share with ceiling 4: about 47% (v2 averaged about 4.9 Hype per scored lot; a ceiling of 4 on the three surviving categories gives about 3.0 against about 3.3 printed). Measure with at least two strategic bots of different strength (L2).

**Termination and length.** Fixed 14 rounds, so the turn cap is 14. Face-down income removes the showing step: expect 15-18 minutes of play plus setup and scoring, about 18-20 minutes against the 20-minute target.

**Skill gap (L6).** Good play beats random in: when to pass versus bid, reading the block (a carried rich block is worth a high card), bidding just under the cards most likely to collide (counted from public cards), choosing which category to burn into (feed your own up to 4, then attack a rival's full category), and steering the crash.

**Every card has a use.** High bids win lots. Low bids win when high bids collide, and are the cheapest way to add Hype without pushing the crash tie-break. The Paddle is income. Every lot is worth at least 2 even after a crash.

**Rule budget (L7).** Still 3 special rules; open income and its no-tableau paragraph are gone. The file is about 145 lines including design notes; the rules a player needs (sections 2-6) are about 85 lines.

**Four players (optional, untested):** remove Bid value 11 of every category (40 Bid cards) and deal 4 each; play all 14 rounds.

**Bot hints for simulation (not rules).**
- *Estimated Hype:* each category's row count plus (rounds left x 0.5); the top estimated category (tie-break as in section 6) is treated as crashing; bonus = min(estimate, 4), 0 for the crash category.
- *Lot worth to me:* printed value + estimated bonus of its category.
- *Unseen cards:* every Bid card not in my hand, not on the discard pile and not in a Hype row; rival hands are sampled from this pool by hand size.
- *Bid choice:* compare passing (worth about 2 cards) with each Bid card: (chance it stands first or second) x (worth of the best lot left for that slot) + (chance it burns) x (burn value) - 1 for the card spent. Burn value = my lots in that category minus 0.5 x rivals' lots in that category while its estimated Hype is below 4; once at 4 or more, burn value = 0.5 x (rivals' lots minus my lots) in that category (crash pressure only).
- *Lot pick:* the lot with the highest worth to me.
- *Random bot:* each round, chooses uniformly among Pass and every Bid card in hand; picks lots uniformly at random.
- *Track per game:* lots left on the block, cancelled bids, cards burned per category, burns into categories already at 4+, which category crashed and whether by tie-break, forced passes, Hype share of points, last-round winner flip, and the provisional leader after each round.

## 8. Changelog

**v3 (revision 2).** Two targeted changes from the critique (REVISE-MINOR, 3.33) and playtest (NEEDS-FIXES) of v2; everything else is unchanged (bids 1-11, 78 cards, lot values 2-5, income 2 per pass, carried lots, crash tie-break, 14 rounds).
- *Hype share 56% (5p) / 64% (6p) against 45-50%, lot values failed as a knob (E1 59%, E4 54% but lone gap 18.0):* each lot now gains **at most +4 from Hype**. The crash is still decided by the uncapped row count. One knob: the ceiling (3/4/5).
- *Open income failed its ablation (no-memory +2.7 / +2.3, needs +5) and is an untested human memory burden:* **cut**. Income is drawn face down; the "no public tableau" paragraph and the shown-again rule are gone. Expected cost from v2 data: lone gap about 23 (no-memory bot 23.1 vs full 25.3).
- *Ignore-ties failed (-7.0 / +2.6):* rule kept, not changed. Reason: it drives carried lots and low-bid wins, which worked; removing it needs a new tie-break rule. The skill claim now rests on public-card counting and will be dropped if the re-test fails (see Known gaps).
- Not done this cycle, to keep the change attributable (L10): the critic's "calm the end" and rules-trim items.

**v2.1 (fix-before-critic pass, wording only).** Closed the 6 playtest ambiguities and cut bids to 1-11 (78 cards) per playtest E3; no balance changes.

**v2 (revision 1).** Open income, bids 1-12, deterministic single crash with tie-break chain, lot values 2-5, income 2 per pass, unsold lots stay on the block; closed the 7 v1 ambiguities.
