# Tug of Crowns - Rules (v2, revision 1)

## 1. Overview

- **Title:** Tug of Crowns
- **Hook:** Two rival court advisors, the steady Treasurer and the sly Whisperer, bid cards in a series of audiences to drag one crown toward their own throne. The court resists whoever is ahead, and once the crown has left the empty throne it can never rest in the middle again: every audience someone wins, someone is ahead.
- **Players:** 2 (one plays the Treasurer, one plays the Whisperer)
- **Play time:** about 15 minutes (7 rounds at most)
- **Age:** 12+
- **Complexity:** 2/5

## 2. Components

47 cards in total. No dice, no board, no tokens required.

- 20 Treasurer cards (gold backs)
- 20 Whisperer cards (violet backs)
- 7 Court track cards
- 2 side reference cards (half A5 each). These are optional; their text is the "Side rules" in section 5. They are not part of any deck.

The crown's position is shown by turning the track card it is on sideways (90 degrees). You may use a coin as the crown marker instead; it changes nothing.

Each deck card shows a name, an **Influence** value (a whole number) and up to two **keywords**. Section 4.5 explains the keywords.

### 2.3 Court track (7 cards)

The track has six **positions**: T3, T2, T1 (the Treasurer's half) and W1, W2, W3 (the Whisperer's half). It also has one **start card**, the Empty Throne, which sits between T1 and W1.

- The **leader** is the player whose half the crown is in. The **trailer** is the other player.
- While the crown is on the Empty Throne, nobody is the leader or the trailer.

| Card | Name | Court rule while the crown is here |
|---|---|---|
| T3 | Treasurer's Throne | The Treasurer wins immediately when the crown arrives here. |
| T2 | Gates of the Throne | In Phase 1 the trailer (Whisperer) draws 2 extra cards. |
| T1 | Murmurs | In Phase 1 the trailer (Whisperer) draws 1 extra card. |
| Start | Empty Throne | The crown starts here. No rule applies. The crown never comes back here (see 4.6). |
| W1 | Murmurs | In Phase 1 the trailer (Treasurer) draws 1 extra card. |
| W2 | Gates of the Throne | In Phase 1 the trailer (Treasurer) draws 2 extra cards. |
| W3 | Whisperer's Throne | The Whisperer wins immediately when the crown arrives here. |

The track is exactly symmetric: each side's cards mirror the other side's.

## 3. Setup

1. Lay out the 7 track cards in a row in this order: T3, T2, T1, Empty Throne, W1, W2, W3. The Treasurer sits at the T3 end and the Whisperer at the W3 end.
2. Turn the Empty Throne sideways. The crown starts there.
3. Decide who plays which side. Either the players agree, or one player shuffles the two reference cards face down and the other player picks one.
4. Each player shuffles their own 20-card deck and puts it face down as their draw pile.
5. Each player draws 6 cards. Hands are hidden from the opponent, but hand sizes are public.
6. Each player leaves room next to the track for a **contest row** (the cards they play this round) and a **discard pile**. The Treasurer also leaves room for a face-down **Treasury**, which starts empty.
7. Begin round 1. The game lasts at most 7 rounds. Count rounds aloud.

## 4. Turn structure

The game is played in **rounds** (at most 7). Each round has four phases, in this order.

### 4.1 Phase 1: Draw

- In round 1, skip this phase (each player already has 6 cards).
- In rounds 2-7, each player draws 2 cards. Then the trailer, if there is one, draws the extra cards given by the court rule of the crown's current position: 1 extra at T1 or W1, 2 extra at T2 or W2. On the Empty Throne nobody draws extra cards.
- If you need to draw and your draw pile is empty, use the **reshuffle** rule (section 5).
- There is no hand limit.

### 4.2 Phase 2: Choose the lead

1. One player is the **chooser**. The chooser decides who takes the first turn of the contest; this player is called the **lead**. The chooser may pick themselves or the opponent.
   - In round 1, the Treasurer is the chooser.
   - In later rounds, the player who lost the previous round is the chooser. If the previous round was tied (see 4.6, step 3), the player who chose in the previous round chooses again.
2. **Lead bonus.** The lead immediately draws 1 card. The lead also wins the round if the totals are equal (see 4.6, step 3).

### 4.3 Phase 3: Contest

Players take turns one after the other, starting with the lead. On your turn you must do exactly one of these things:

- **Play a card.** Put one card from your hand face up in your contest row. Resolve its keywords immediately (section 4.5).
- **Pass.** If you have no cards in your hand, you must pass.

Passing is final for the round. A player who has passed takes no more turns this round. The only exception is the Whisperer's Retort (section 4.4).

When one player has passed, the other player keeps taking turns alone (one card per turn) until they pass too. The contest ends as soon as both players have passed.

### 4.4 Retort timing (Whisperer only)

- Retort matters only after the Whisperer has passed. Before the Whisperer passes, a Retort card is played like any other card, on the Whisperer's own turn.
- After the Whisperer has passed, each time the Treasurer plays a card (and its keywords have resolved), the Whisperer may play **one** Retort card from hand, or decline. The Retort card's keywords resolve when it is played. Then the Treasurer takes the next turn.
- After a Retort the Whisperer is still passed. The Whisperer does not need to pass again and cannot take a normal turn.
- A Retort can only follow a card the Treasurer plays. It can never follow a Treasurer pass. So once both players have passed, the contest ends immediately.
- If the Treasurer passes first, the Whisperer simply keeps taking normal turns until the Whisperer passes. Retort timing never comes up in that round.

### 4.5 Keywords

Treasurer keywords:

- **Steady.** A Hush cannot target this card.
- **Bank.** In Phase 4 cleanup, this card goes face down into the Treasury instead of the discard pile, even if it was Hushed. Each card in the Treasury is one **coin**; its face no longer matters. The Treasury holds at most 3 cards. If it is full, any extra Bank card goes to the discard pile instead.
- **Spend X.** When you play this card, choose to discard 0 to X cards from your Treasury to your discard pile (at most the number of coins you have). This card gains +2 Influence for this round for each card you discarded this way. You choose the number when you play the card, and you cannot change it later.

Whisperer keywords:

- **Hush.** When you play this card, choose one card in the Treasurer's contest row that is not Steady and is not already Hushed. Turn it sideways: its Influence counts as 0 for the rest of the round, including any Spend bonus. Coins already spent on that Spend card stay in the discard pile; they are not refunded. If no card is a legal target, Hush does nothing, and the Hush card still counts its own Influence.
- **Retort.** You may play this card after you have passed, following the timing in section 4.4.
- **Echo.** In Phase 4 cleanup, if the Whisperer did **not** win this round (the Whisperer lost, or the round was tied), this card goes back to the Whisperer's hand instead of the discard pile.

### 4.6 Phase 4: Resolve and cleanup

1. **Totals.** Each player adds up the Influence of every card in their contest row. Hushed cards count 0 and Spend bonuses count. An empty row totals 0.
2. **Margin.** The margin is the higher total minus the lower total.
3. **Round winner.** The player with the higher total wins the round. If the totals are equal, the lead wins the round. The only exception is when both totals are 0: then the round is **tied** and nobody wins.
4. **Move the crown.** If there is a winner, move the crown toward the winner's throne:
   - **1 step** if the margin is 0 to 4;
   - **2 steps** if the margin is 5 or more.

   Count steps along the six positions in order: T3, T2, T1, W1, W2, W3. **The Empty Throne is never counted and the crown never returns to it.** From T1, one step toward the Whisperer lands on W1. From the Empty Throne (the start), 1 step lands on T1 or W1 and 2 steps land on T2 or W2. The crown stops at a throne even if steps are left over. If the round is tied, the crown does not move. Turn the new card sideways and straighten the old one.
5. **Throne check.** If the crown reached T3 or W3, that player wins immediately and the game ends.
6. **Cleanup.**
   - Bank cards go to the Treasury, up to its cap of 3. If more Bank cards arrive than there is room for, the Treasurer chooses which ones go in; the rest go to the discard pile.
   - If the Whisperer did not win, Echo cards go back to the Whisperer's hand.
   - All other contest cards go to their owners' discard piles. Straighten Hushed cards as they leave the row.
7. If this was round 7, the game ends (section 6). Otherwise begin the next round.

## 5. Special rules and timing

### Side rules (the text on each half-A5 reference card)

**Treasurer: "Coin wins the court."** Keywords: Steady, Bank, Spend X (see 4.5). The Treasury holds at most 3 face-down coins, and its size is public.

**Whisperer: "A word in the right ear."** Keywords: Hush, Retort, Echo (see 4.5). After you pass, you may Retort once after each card the Treasurer plays.

### Timing and conflicts

- **Order inside a play.** The card goes into the contest row, then its keywords resolve in printed order. Spend is chosen as the card enters the row; Hush picks its target as the card enters the row. Retort only controls when the card can be played.
- **Hushing a card with Retort.** After the Treasurer plays a card, a passed Whisperer may Retort with False Witness and Hush that same card, if it is not Steady. Then the Treasurer takes the next turn and may play again or pass.
- **Hush targets** must already be in the Treasurer's contest row. A Hush never affects a card played later.
- **Court rules** are read from the crown's position at the start of Phase 1. The crown cannot move during Phases 1-3.
- **Reshuffle.** Whenever you must draw and your draw pile is empty, shuffle your discard pile; it becomes your new draw pile, and you keep drawing. Cards in your hand, your contest row and the Treasury are never part of the reshuffle. If your discard pile is also empty, any remaining draws are lost.
- **Empty Treasury.** Spend cards can still be played; they simply get no bonus.
- **Empty hand.** A player with no cards in hand must pass. Echo cards that return to hand can be played in the next round as usual.
- **Hidden and public information.** Hands and draw piles are hidden. Contest rows, discard piles, hand sizes, draw pile sizes and the Treasury size are public. Either player may look through any discard pile at any time.
- **Randomness.** The only random elements are the shuffles: each deck at setup, and each reshuffle.

## 6. End of game and scoring

The game ends at the first of these moments:

1. **Throne.** The crown reaches T3 (the Treasurer wins) or W3 (the Whisperer wins) in Phase 4, step 5.
2. **End of round 7.** The leader (the player whose half the crown is in) wins.

**Tiebreaker.** The crown can only be on the Empty Throne after round 7 if every round was tied (both totals 0 in every round). In that case the Whisperer wins, because the Treasurer was the first chooser.

There are no points. The result is a win or a loss.

## 7. Design notes

### Intended play and tensions

- **Every turn is an auction step.** Playing a card raises your bid; passing locks it in. The player who passes second has the last word but must spend cards to win. The core decision every turn is: commit another card now, or save it for a round that matters more? Winning by exactly enough is efficient. Winning by 5 or more moves the crown twice, which is the fast way to a throne or a big swing back.
- **The lead is now a real choice.** Going second gives you the last word. Going first gives you an extra card and wins equal totals, so your opponent has to beat your total, not just match it. The chooser is the player who lost the last round, so the catch-up choice always goes to the player who needs it. The choice depends on context: the Whisperer can lead and still get a last word through Retort, so leading costs the Whisperer less. When the Treasurer leads, any non-Steady card it plays is open to a Retorted Hush. Low hands and empty draw piles make the extra card worth more.
- **Treasurer (steady, resources).** It has high raw Influence (58 printed against 42). Bank cards become coins that pay for big Spend plays later, so the Treasurer plans a round or two ahead. Steady cards are the safe bids when the Whisperer is holding Hushes.
- **Whisperer (tricky, reactive).** It has low raw Influence, but Hush deletes Treasurer cards and Retort lets the Whisperer pass as a bluff and strike after the Treasurer commits. Echo cards come back after a round the Whisperer did not win, so it can throw cheap rounds and fight the important ones.
- **Reading the opponent.** Both players can look through the other's discard pile, so it pays to remember what is left before the next reshuffle.

### The twist: the court fights back, and the middle does not exist

The track cards are not just spaces. The nearer the crown gets to a throne, the more cards the court deals the trailer. And once the crown leaves the Empty Throne it never returns, so every round someone wins either pushes the leader on or hands the lead to the other player. The track punishes standing still: a single won round from T1 or W1 is a lead change. The track gives both sides the same rules, so it adds no asymmetry that would need balancing.

### Balance approach: symmetric power, asymmetric means

Both decks are built to the same budget, about 60 "effective Influence" over a game. The Treasurer's comes mostly from printed values (58) plus coins. The Whisperer's comes from 42 printed plus Hush, Echo recycling and the timing of Retort. The track, setup and round structure are the same for both sides.

**Balance levers, in the order we suggest tuning them.** Change one at a time.

1. **Lead bonus.** If the lead wins more than 60% of rounds in the mirror, remove the tie-win and keep the extra card. If the lead wins less than 40%, make the bonus 2 cards.
2. **Spend bonus per coin** (+2). Going to +1 weakens the Treasurer a lot; this is the coarse lever.
3. **Treasury cap** (3). Raising it to 4 helps the Treasurer; lowering it to 2 hurts the Treasurer.
4. **Number of Steady cards** (7). Adding or removing Steady on the 3 Ledgers changes how many Hush targets exist.
5. **Hush count** (6). Swap one Silencer for a Courtier, or back.
6. **Echo condition** ("did not win"). Changing it to "always" strengthens the Whisperer a lot.
7. **Single-card values (fine tuning).** Gold Purse (Treasurer, 4) and Courtier (Whisperer, 3) are the plus-or-minus-1 dials. Each step changes that deck's printed total by 3.
8. **Round 1 chooser** (Treasurer). Give it to the Whisperer if the Treasurer is ahead by a point or two.
9. **Court strength (shared).** If leaders run away or too many games end on a throne, raise the Gates draw to 3 or the double-move margin to 6. If games stall, lower the Gates draw to 1.

### Notes for the playtester (bot coding)

- **Legal actions on a turn:** pass, or play any card in hand. For Spend, choose 0 to min(X, Treasury size) coins. For Hush, choose one legal target, or none if there is no legal target. After each Treasurer play, a passed Whisperer also chooses: Retort with card R, or decline.
- **Lead choice:** the chooser picks "lead myself" or "opponent leads". The strategic bot should weigh both options rather than use a fixed rule. Please report the lead's round-win rate, and the game win rate of each fixed lead policy against an evaluating bot.
- **Lead change:** count one each time the crown moves from one half to the other (T-side to W-side or back). The first move off the Empty Throne does not count.
- **Suggested simple strategic heuristic:** keep a target margin for the round. That target is the minimum needed to win, or 5 if a 2-step move wins the game or crosses the centre. Play the cheapest card that keeps you at or above the target, and pass otherwise. Save Hush and Spend cards for rounds where the crown is at a Gates position or a 5+ margin would decide the game.
- **Watch for:** lead changes (target 2 or more), endings by type (throne, round-7 leader, all-tied tiebreak), the double-move rate, average rounds played, reshuffles per game per side, and the side gap for random, greedy and strategic mirrors.
