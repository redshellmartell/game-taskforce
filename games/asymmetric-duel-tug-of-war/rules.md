# Tug of Crowns - Rules (v3, revision 2)

## 1. Overview

- **Title:** Tug of Crowns
- **Hook:** Two rival court advisors, the steady Treasurer and the sly Whisperer, bid cards in a series of audiences to drag one crown toward their own throne. The court resists whoever is ahead, and once the crown has left the empty throne it can never rest in the middle again: every audience someone wins, someone is ahead.
- **Players:** 2 (one plays the Treasurer, one plays the Whisperer)
- **Play time:** about 15 minutes (7 rounds at most)
- **Age:** 12+
- **Complexity:** 2/5

## 2. Components

47 cards in total. No dice, no board, no tokens required.

- 20 Treasurer cards (gold backs)
- 20 Whisperer cards (violet backs)
- 7 Court track cards
- 2 side reference cards (half A5 each). These are optional; their text is the "Side rules" in section 5. They are not part of any deck.

The crown's position is shown by turning the track card it is on sideways (90 degrees). You may use a coin as the crown marker instead; it changes nothing.

Each deck card shows a name, an **Influence** value (a whole number) and up to two **keywords**. Section 4.5 explains the keywords.

### 2.3 Court track (7 cards)

The track has six **positions**: T3, T2, T1 (the Treasurer's half) and W1, W2, W3 (the Whisperer's half). It also has one **start card**, the Empty Throne, which sits between T1 and W1.

- The **leader** is the player whose half the crown is in. The **trailer** is the other player.
- While the crown is on the Empty Throne, nobody is the leader or the trailer.

| Card | Name | Court rule while the crown is here |
|---|---|---|

The track is exactly symmetric: each side's cards mirror the other side's.

## 3. Setup

1. Lay out the 7 track cards in a row in this order: T3, T2, T1, Empty Throne, W1, W2, W3. The Treasurer sits at the T3 end and the Whisperer at the W3 end.
2. Turn the Empty Throne sideways. The crown starts there.
3. Decide who plays which side. Either the players agree, or one player shuffles the two reference cards face down and the other player picks one.
4. Each player shuffles their own 20-card deck and puts it face down as their draw pile.
5. Each player draws 6 cards. Hands are hidden from the opponent, but hand sizes are public.
6. Each player leaves room next to the track for a **contest row** (the cards they play this round) and a **discard pile**. The Treasurer also leaves room for a face-down **Treasury**, which starts empty.
7. Begin round 1. The game lasts at most 7 rounds. Count rounds aloud.

## 4. Turn structure

The game is played in **rounds** (at most 7). Each round has four phases, in this order.

### 4.1 Phase 1: Draw

- In round 1, skip this phase (each player already has 6 cards).
- In rounds 2-7, each player draws 2 cards. Then the trailer, if there is one, draws the extra cards given by the court rule of the crown's current position: 1 extra at T1 or W1, 2 extra at T2 or W2. On the Empty Throne nobody draws extra cards.
- If you need to draw and your draw pile is empty, use the **reshuffle** rule (section 5).
- There is no hand limit.

### 4.2 Phase 2: Set the lead

There is no choice here. The **lead** is the player who takes the first turn of the contest:

- In round 1, the Treasurer leads.
- In rounds 2-7, the player who **lost the previous round** leads. If the previous round was tied (both totals 0, see 4.6 step 3), the same player leads again.

The lead gets no extra card. Its only privilege is that it wins the round when the totals are equal and not both 0 (4.6 step 3).

### 4.3 Phase 3: Contest

Players take turns one after the other, starting with the lead. On your turn you must do exactly one of these things:

- **Play a card.** Put one card from your hand face up in your contest row. Resolve its keywords immediately (section 4.5).
- **Pass.** If you have no cards in your hand, you must pass.

Passing is final for the round. A player who has passed takes no more turns this round. The only exception is the Whisperer's Retort (section 4.4).

When one player has passed, the other player keeps taking turns alone (one card per turn) until they pass too. The contest ends as soon as both players have passed.

### 4.4 Retort timing (Whisperer only)

- Retort matters only after the Whisperer has passed. Before the Whisperer passes, a Retort card is played like any other card, on the Whisperer's own turn.
- After the Whisperer has passed, each time the Treasurer plays a card (and its keywords have resolved), the Whisperer may play **one** Retort card from hand, or decline. The Retort card's keywords resolve when it is played. Then the Treasurer takes the next turn.
- After a Retort the Whisperer is still passed. The Whisperer does not need to pass again and cannot take a normal turn.
- A Retort can only follow a card the Treasurer plays. It can never follow a Treasurer pass. So once both players have passed, the contest ends immediately.
- If the Treasurer passes first, the Whisperer simply keeps taking normal turns until the Whisperer passes. Retort timing never comes up in that round.

### 4.5 Keywords

Treasurer keywords:

- **Steady.** A Hush cannot target this card.
- **Bank.** In Phase 4 cleanup, this card goes face down into the Treasury instead of the discard pile, even if it was Hushed. Each card in the Treasury is one **coin**; its face no longer matters. **The Treasury holds at most 2 cards.** If it is full, any extra Bank card goes to the discard pile instead.
- **Spend X.** When you play this card, choose to discard 0 to X cards from your Treasury to your discard pile (never more than the number of coins you have). This card gains +2 Influence for this round for each card you discarded this way. You choose the number when you play the card, and you cannot change it later.

Whisperer keywords:

- **Hush.** When you play this card, choose one card in the Treasurer's contest row that is not Steady and is not already Hushed. Turn it sideways: its Influence counts as 0 for the rest of the round, including any Spend bonus. Coins already spent on that Spend card stay in the discard pile; they are not refunded. If no card is a legal target, Hush does nothing, and the Hush card still counts its own Influence.
- **Retort.** You may play this card after you have passed, following the timing in section 4.4.
- **Echo.** In Phase 4 cleanup, if the Whisperer did **not** win this round (the Whisperer lost, or the round was tied), this card goes back to the Whisperer's hand instead of the discard pile.

### 4.6 Phase 4: Resolve and cleanup

1. **Totals.** Each player adds up the Influence of every card in their contest row. Hushed cards count 0 and Spend bonuses count. An empty row totals 0.
2. **Margin.** The margin is the higher total minus the lower total.
3. **Round winner.** The player with the higher total wins the round. If the totals are equal, the lead wins the round. The only exception is when both totals are 0: then the round is **tied** and nobody wins.
4. **Move the crown.** If there is a winner, move the crown toward the winner's throne:
   - **1 step** if the margin is 0 to 5;
   - **2 steps** if the margin is 6 or more.

   Count steps along the six positions in order: T3, T2, T1, W1, W2, W3. **The Empty Throne is never counted and the crown never returns to it.** From T1, one step toward the Whisperer lands on W1. From the Empty Throne (the start), 1 step lands on T1 or W1 and 2 steps land on T2 or W2. The crown stops at a throne even if steps are left over. If the round is tied, the crown does not move. Turn the new card sideways and straighten the old one.
5. **Throne check.** If the crown reached T3 or W3, that player wins immediately and the game ends.
6. **Cleanup.**
   - Bank cards go to the Treasury, up to its cap of 2. If more Bank cards arrive than there is room for, the Treasurer chooses which ones go in; the rest go to the discard pile.
   - If the Whisperer did not win, Echo cards go back to the Whisperer's hand.
   - All other contest cards go to their owners' discard piles. Straighten Hushed cards as they leave the row.
7. If this was round 7, the game ends (section 6). Otherwise begin the next round.

## 5. Special rules and timing

### Side rules (the text on each half-A5 reference card)

**Treasurer: "Coin wins the court."** Keywords: Steady, Bank, Spend X (see 4.5). The Treasury holds at most 2 face-down coins, and its size is public.

**Whisperer: "A word in the right ear."** Keywords: Hush, Retort, Echo (see 4.5). After you pass, you may Retort once after each card the Treasurer plays.

### Timing and conflicts

- **Order inside a play.** The card goes into the contest row, then its keywords resolve in printed order. Spend is chosen as the card enters the row; Hush picks its target as the card enters the row. Retort only controls when the card can be played.
- **Hushing a card with Retort.** After the Treasurer plays a card, a passed Whisperer may Retort with False Witness and Hush that same card, if it is not Steady. Then the Treasurer takes the next turn and may play again or pass.
- **Hush targets** must already be in the Treasurer's contest row. A Hush never affects a card played later.
- **Court rules and the lead** are read at the start of Phase 1 and Phase 2 from the crown's position and the previous round's result. The crown cannot move during Phases 1-3.
- **Spend over the cap.** Treasury Vault (Spend 3) can discard at most 2 coins, because the Treasury never holds more than 2.
- **Reshuffle.** Whenever you must draw and your draw pile is empty, shuffle your discard pile; it becomes your new draw pile, and you keep drawing. Cards in your hand, your contest row and the Treasury are never part of the reshuffle. Spent coins are ordinary discarded cards and are reshuffled with their printed faces. If your discard pile is also empty, any remaining draws are lost.
- **Empty Treasury.** Spend cards can still be played; they simply get no bonus.
- **Empty hand.** A player with no cards in hand must pass. Echo cards that return to hand can be played in the next round as usual.
- **Hidden and public information.** Hands and draw piles are hidden. Contest rows, discard piles, hand sizes, draw pile sizes and the Treasury size are public. Either player may look through any discard pile at any time.
- **Randomness.** The only random elements are the shuffles: each deck at setup, and each reshuffle.

## 6. End of game and scoring

The game ends at the first of these moments:

1. **Throne.** The crown reaches T3 (the Treasurer wins) or W3 (the Whisperer wins) in Phase 4, step 5.
2. **End of round 7.** The leader (the player whose half the crown is in) wins.

**Tiebreaker.** The crown can only be on the Empty Throne after round 7 if every round was tied (both totals 0 in every round). In that case the Whisperer wins, because the Treasurer led round 1.

There are no points. The result is a win or a loss. **Turn cap:** the game cannot exceed 7 rounds; a round cannot exceed 40 card plays in total (each play removes a card from a hand, and only Echo returns cards, at cleanup), so it always terminates.

## 7. Design notes

### Intended play and tensions

- **Every turn is an auction step.** Playing a card raises your bid; passing locks it in. The player who passes second has the last word but must spend cards to win. The core decision every turn is: commit another card now, or save it for a round that matters more? Winning by exactly enough is efficient. Winning by 6 or more moves the crown twice.
- **The lead is fixed, not chosen.** The loser of the last round leads and wins equal totals. That is a small catch-up edge (ties go to the player who just lost) with no decision attached, so it cannot be "solved". The Whisperer still has a last word through Retort even when leading; the Treasurer, when leading, exposes non-Steady cards to a Retorted Hush.
- **Treasurer (steady, resources).** High raw Influence (58 against 42). Bank cards become coins (at most 2 banked) that pay for Spend plays later, so the Treasurer plans a round ahead. Steady cards are the safe bids when the Whisperer holds Hushes.
- **Whisperer (tricky, reactive).** Low raw Influence, but Hush deletes Treasurer cards and Retort lets the Whisperer pass as a bluff and strike after the Treasurer commits. Echo cards come back after a round the Whisperer did not win, so it can throw cheap rounds and fight the important ones.

### The twist: the court fights back, and the middle does not exist

The nearer the crown gets to a throne, the more cards the court deals the trailer. Once the crown leaves the Empty Throne it never returns, so a single won round from T1 or W1 is a lead change.

### Self-check (studio playbook)

1. **Comeback (L3).** Three mechanisms: the court draws (trailer +1 at position 1, +2 at position 2), Echo for a losing Whisperer, and the round loser leading and winning equal totals. Round 1 cannot decide the game: the best round 1 result is position 2 (one step from a throne), which then gives the trailer +2 cards. KPIs: runaway leader at most 65%, lead changes at least 2.0, games ending by round 3 under 20% (v2: 31%). Raising the double-move margin to 6 is the change aimed at the last two.
2. **Twist and keyword ablation (L1).** Each ablation bot plays the strategic bot's policy minus one feature and must lose at least 5 points against the full strategic bot on the same side: (a) Treasurer never Spends (plays Spend cards for printed value only); (b) Treasurer ignores Steady when choosing between equal plays; (c) Whisperer never Hushes a target (plays Hush cards for value, target chosen at random); (d) Whisperer never passes early to Retort; (e) Whisperer never deliberately throws a round to recover Echo. For the court draws, run the full rules with all extra draws set to 0 and report runaway and lead changes; the court must move lead changes by at least 0.3.
3. **Dead cards and ambiguities (L4), fixes made in this pass.** (i) The lead choice and lead extra card are removed, so "who chooses after a tie" and "when the extra card is drawn" no longer exist. (ii) Treasury Vault with a cap of 2 can spend at most 2 coins; stated in section 5. It still beats Royal Loan (same maximum, plus Steady), so it is not dead. (iii) Spent coins return to circulation as normal cards on a reshuffle (the simulator bug in revision 1 put them back as blanks); stated in section 5. (iv) The all-tied tiebreaker reason is reworded for the fixed lead. No card is known to be never the best play; The Whisper is strong (see Known gaps).
4. **Target band and one tuning knob (L5, L8).** Two-player only. Band: Treasurer win rate 45-55% in the strategic mirror, side gap at most 5 there and at most 10 across random, greedy and strategic mirrors. **Knob: the Treasury cap** (now 2, was 3). If the strategic-mirror Treasurer is below 45%, set it back to 3; if above 55% at cap 2, the next lever is the Spend bonus (+2 to +1 per coin). Change one lever per revision.
5. **Termination and length.** At most 7 rounds; each round ends because every play empties a hand slot (turn cap above). Expected about 4.5-5.5 rounds and 13-16 minutes against the brief's 15 (v2 was 4.4 rounds, 12.7 minutes; margin 6 should lengthen it).
6. **Rule budget (L7).** About 190 lines in this file, of which sections 2-6 are about 130; 6 keywords plus 1 Retort timing rule. Over the brief's "one page, at most 3 special rules" promise (Known gaps).
7. **Skill gap (L6).** v2 measured +56 points strategic over random. Carried by: when to pass, Hush targeting, Retort bluffs, banking versus spending, and throwing Echo rounds. Target stays at least 20.

### Balance levers (one at a time, in this order)

1. **Treasury cap** (now 2). Back to 3 helps the Treasurer.
2. **Spend bonus per coin** (+2). +1 weakens the Treasurer a lot; coarse.
3. **The Whisper** (Influence 5). Drop to 4 if it stays a large outlier.
4. **Number of Steady cards** (7), **Hush count** (6), **Echo condition** ("did not win"): coarse asymmetry levers.
5. **Single-card values:** Gold Purse (Treasurer, 4) and Courtier (Whisperer, 3), each step changes a deck's printed total by 3.
6. **Round 1 lead** (Treasurer). Give it to the Whisperer if the Treasurer is ahead by a point or two.
7. **Court strength (shared).** If leaders run away, raise the Gates draw to 3; if games stall, lower it to 1.

### Notes for the playtester (bot coding)

- **Legal actions on a turn:** pass, or play any card in hand. For Spend, choose 0 to min(X, Treasury size) coins. For Hush, choose one legal target, or none if there is no legal target. After each Treasurer play, a passed Whisperer also chooses: Retort with card R, or decline. There is no lead decision.
- **Lead change:** count one each time the crown moves from one half to the other. The first move off the Empty Throne does not count.
- **Please report:** side gap for random, greedy and strategic mirrors separately; lead changes; runaway leader; games ending by round 3; endings by type; double-move rate; average rounds; lead's round-win rate; reshuffles per side; the ablation results in item 2 above. All on the corrected simulator (coins reshuffle as real cards).
