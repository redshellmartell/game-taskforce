# Duel Flip: test panel report (first run, revision 1)

Source: `panel.json` (free bot scores, 10 tables, 1,600 games per persona) and five Haiku reviews in `panel/`. Advisory only; the owner's own playtests are the real check.

## Who it is for
Casual and Competitor players rate it highest (fun 4/5 each); Casual would buy at $20 and calls it a quick, tense push-your-luck filler. Strategist (3/5) likes the quick teach but wants more to think about. Story and Family (3/5 each) are the weakest fit: Story finds the theme pasted on and the game too short for an arc, Family dislikes early bust streaks.

## Where they agree
- Rules are clear and quick to teach; nobody listed a confusing rule.
- 10-15 minutes is right for a filler, and the two-player tension is good.
- All five are "maybe" or "yes" to buy, at $20-25 (mean review fun 3.4 against 3.82 predicted from the numbers).

## Where they differ
- Casual: no peeves hit. Competitor and Strategist: "leave your lowest card" is a solved line, so replay drops (replay 3 and 4 against 3.98 and 3.89 predicted).
- Family had the highest fell-behind rate (36.6% in the bot games) and names busts early in the game as its frustration. Story's frustration is the interchangeable theme.
- Biggest gap from scores: reviews average about 0.4 below the predicted fun, mostly from Strategist, Competitor and Story. Prices came out 5-12 USD below predicted for Competitor and Story.

## Recurring complaints
1. "Leave lowest" dominates the bait decision (named by three personas, matching the playtester's flag).
2. Early bust streaks feel unrecoverable for weaker players.
3. Theme could be swapped without changing play.

## Suggested changes
- Make the bait decision matter (all five suggest it): for example let a bait hit pay more, per the playtester's earlier fix idea.
- Soften early busts for new players (free Lifebuoy on a one-card pile).
- Optional: give the creatures a rule each, so the theme has weight.

## About this run
The five reviews share one weakness: the "one change" and "recommend to" lines are almost identical across personas, because `rules.md` has a design-notes section that names the "leave lowest" worry. Reviews also ran over the 350-word limit (414-759 words). Personas still differ in their frustration and ratings; a future version should keep design notes out of the files a persona reads.
