# Duel Flip - Critique (rules v3, revision 3 review)

**Verdict: PASS** (narrow, with a documented KPI miss). Average 3.67, above the 3.5 pitch bar.

Revision 3 did what two earlier revisions did not: the bait is now a real decision. The 2x hurdle moved bait claim rates for 8/9/10 to 65/39/17%. A smart leaver picks a non-lowest card in 27.9% of choices. Leave-lowest (44.7%) and leave-highest (46.5%) both lose to it, and neither beats the other. The runaway leader rate (74.0% against 65%) is the one KPI still missed. It has not moved in three revisions and is documented as known.

## Originality (small check)

I ran one plain web search for a "leave one card as bait, claim at double value" mechanic in a 2-player shared-row game. It found only Fairy Lights, Push and similar push-your-luck games, with no match for this mechanic. That is a weak absence signal, not proof: I did not search card-by-card, and I did not check BoardGameGeek, per the owner's rule.

- The core loop is still Port Royal (shared display, duplicate ends the turn) combined with Flip 7 (duplicate number busts). That has not changed.
- The bait hurdle is the one differentiator. The mechanic of leaving a card the rival must double to claim has no direct match in what I found. It does not change the base loop, but it is now visible in play, which it was not in rev 2.
- Score 3: not copied, and not close enough to kill, but the pitch must say "Port Royal and Flip 7 neighbours with a bait twist". It must not call the river new.

## Biggest strength
The bait hurdle now creates a real, readable decision: low baits are safe gifts, a 9 or 10 is a bet the rival often cannot make, and a failed push costs the rival their next bait. All of that is public information with no cards in hand, and it costs almost nothing to build.

## Biggest weakness
The runaway leader rate is 74.0% (target 65%), unchanged across three revisions, so trailing players often are out of it. The bait gains are also modest: a few points per head-to-head, and measured only against a one-step-lookahead bot.

## Balance against the KPIs
- Pass: seat gap 2.0 (5 or less), skill gap 38.1 (20 or more), length about 12 min (within brief), lead changes 2.80 (2 or more), zero dead cards, zero ambiguities, critic average 3.67 (3.5 or more).
- Fail: runaway leader 74.0% (65% or less). Known and disclosed.
- Caveat on the bait result: the bot under-plans pushes to 18-20. Humans may claim 9-10 baits more often (the sim says 39% and 17%), which would push leave-lowest back toward correct. The 1.5x knob (rounded up) is ready if so. This can only be settled by human play.

## Required changes
None required for PASS. For the physical prototype stage, treat these as things to watch:
1. Human playtests should record whether the trailing player feels out of it at the midpoint. This targets the runaway KPI (74.0%). If it does, test one catch-up rule only (trailing player claims at 1.5x, or a smaller bust loss).
2. Watch whether humans claim 9-10 baits more often than the sim's 39% and 17%. If leave-lowest becomes dominant, switch to the 1.5x claim (rounded up).
3. Watch whether players read the "no new bait after a failed claim" clause as fair.

## Recommendation to the owner: pitch (with risks)
Pitch it as a cheap, fair 2-player push-your-luck filler with a bait-hurdle twist. The prototype is 60 cards and 2 tokens, and the main questions need humans, not more simulation. The risks the pitch must state:
1. Runaway leader 74.0% against a 65% target (a known, unfixed miss).
2. The bait payoff is modest and was measured with a one-step bot; humans may change it.
3. It sits next to Port Royal and Flip 7 and needs the twist to justify itself. No BoardGameGeek check was made (owner rule), and the web search was thin.
4. Panel fun is 3.60, and the game is a weaker fit for Family and Story players.

Park is reasonable if the owner wants more than a filler, but nothing here warrants it. KILL is not warranted: nothing is copied, and it is fair, short and nearly free to build.

## Is another revision worth it?
No: the 3-loop limit is reached, the bait is fixed, and the one remaining miss (runaway leader) has resisted three revisions and is better judged by human play than by more simulation.
