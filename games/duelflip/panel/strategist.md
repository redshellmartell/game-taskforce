# Duel Flip: The Strategist's Review

## First impression

The rules are clean and teach in under five minutes—I respect that clarity. The hook is strong: shared river, mandatory second flip, bait that you reclaim if an opponent crashes into it. It's tight. But 10-15 minutes is the opposite of my preferred range, and on first read the "leave one card behind" rule feels like a trap: "leave lowest own card" looks dominant.

## Best moment and worst moment

**Best:** Turn 4 against Story (best table), I had Cr1 clashing and chose to spend my Lifebuoy rather than bust. That was a real decision—weighing the cost of the Lifebuoy against the pile I'd already flipped. The turn where I then banked just my bait and reset felt like I was executing a half-phase plan.

**Worst:** Turn 0 and Turn 2 in that same game. Safe first flip, fine. Then I flip Ur3 (clash) immediately, and opponent gets my haul. Then two turns later, Ke4 (clash again). Early game variance hit me hard, and there was nothing clever I could have done. That is output randomness undoing my decision, and it stung.

## Confusing rules

The bait rule is actually clear once you read it twice: if an opponent busts on the card you left behind, you reclaim it along with their entire pile. The timing confused me at first, but the log makes it work. No real issues with wording.

## Pet peeves hit

**Solved strategy (pet peeve #4).** The design notes themselves admit they are "watching whether 'leave lowest own card' is still dominant." In both logs I read, the left card is almost always the lowest or close to it. Every banking decision is "take all my flips, leave the worst one." That is not a decision; that is following the rulebook. If I cannot see *why* I would leave a higher card, the planning evaporates. The species bonus and majority scoring mitigate this slightly—sometimes you leave a species to deny me—but the first instinct is still "lowest, always."

## Who I enjoyed playing with, and who I didn't

**Best table (vs. Story):** Story played exploratory, willing to bust, building smaller piles. That kept the river alive and meant every clash mattered. Turn 3 when Story banked a four-card haul was the first real "oh, that's the play" moment in the game. I felt under pressure to match aggression without busting myself.

**Worst table (vs. Competitor):** Competitor executed a solved line crisply. Early 4-card bank to refund the Lifebuoy, then small conservative two-flips the rest of the way. I pushed harder to contest it, banked some bigger piles (T2, T5), and still lost. Competitor played the math correctly and I could see it. That's not a fun difference—Competitor just understood the game faster.

## Ratings

**Fun: 3/5.** It's a tight dueling game and the bait mechanic is novel, but the push-your-luck core is not my play style, and the dominant "leave lowest" decision drains the satisfaction. I was rarely surprised by what the right move was. Comparable to **Wingspan** (3/5)—pleasant mechanics and good two-player interaction, but close to a puzzle over a contest.

**Replay: 4/5.** Two-player is the correct count. Each game is different (shuffle varies, clashes vary). I would play it again, especially as a quick opener before a heavier game. But I would not return to it often.

**Would buy: Yes, at $25–$35.** It's a fair price for a 10-minute dueler. I will not treasure it, but I will not regret owning it. $29 is right in my range.

## The one change I'd make

The "leave lowest" dominant strategy has to break. Either:
1. Let players choose to *leave* a leftover from the river (as you initially designed), so the bait is a real threat and the decision has branches, or
2. Add a scoring track where flipping higher-value cards or specific sequences gives bonus points, so you sometimes *want* to leave something with upside to deny me.

The current rule—"take all leftovers"—forces the hand into routine play. Every other decision in the game has tension; this one should too.

## Who I'd recommend this to

**Yes:** 
- Two-player specialists looking for a quick dueler (15 min, no table setup).
- People who like push-your-luck games and can tolerate some early-game variance.

**No:**
- Strategists wanting a full evening or even a meaty mid-length game.
- Players who hate luck; the safe first flip helps, but busts still sting.
- Anyone seeking a "solved" game. (This one is half-solved, which is worse than unsolved.)
