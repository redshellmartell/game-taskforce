# Duel Flip Review: The Casual Social

## First impression
The rules are clean and teach in under five minutes. The 10-15 minute play time is exactly my sweet spot, and two players with a shared river full of push-your-luck tension? That's the kind of game you put on the table without reading a manual.

## Best moment and worst moment
**Best:** Turn 6 of my game versus Story. I flipped cautiously, landed two cards without clashing, banked three while leaving my lowest as bait—setting a small trap that created real tension.

**Worst:** Turn 10 versus Strategist. I flipped safely, then clashed against their bait from earlier and busted my pile. Bad luck, but it proved the game has stakes and swings that matter.

## Confusing rules
None. The turn structure is crystal clear: safe first flip (can't bust), mandatory second flip, then stop or push, then bank (take all but one). The pile/leftover distinction made sense once I played a round.

## Pet peeves hit
None. No downtime (two players, quick turns), perfect play time, rules so simple they teach themselves, no waiting, no elimination, no fiddly icons.

## Who I enjoyed playing with
My best game was versus Story. We felt like two people figuring it out together, creating real "do I risk it?" moments. My worst game was versus Strategist, who played so tightly they won 73% of matchups—still fun, but I felt outmaneuvered by perfect play rather than enjoying an even contest.

## Ratings
**Fun: 4/5** – Splendor-level fun: quick teach, quick play, genuinely tense moments. Not a 5 because most decisions feel reactive (push or stop?) rather than clever reads of an opponent.

**Replay: 4/5** – I'd absolutely play this again multiple times. At 10-15 minutes, you could run 2-3 games in one night.

**Would buy: Yes, $20** – It's an easy purchase: zero setup, 60 cards, teaches in a minute, plays in 15. A filler game that feels thoughtful enough to matter.

## The one change I'd make
Let players choose which of their pile cards to leave as bait, not just the lowest. Right now leaving your lowest is nearly automatic, which kills a micro-decision that could create bluffing and opponent-reading instead.

## Who I'd recommend this to
Anyone wanting a two-player game they can teach in a minute and play in 15 minutes flat. People who love Port Royal or Flip 7. NOT for strategists wanting hidden depths or a night-long game, but for casual game nights it's perfect.
