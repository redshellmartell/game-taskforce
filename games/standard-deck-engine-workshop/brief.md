# Brief: Fifty-Two Workshop

- **Working title / slug:** Fifty-Two Workshop / `standard-deck-engine-workshop`
- **Idea-bank id:** `standard-deck-engine-workshop` (scan of 2026-10-03)

## Target
- **Players:** 1-4 (solo mode is a first-class mode, not an afterthought)
- **Play time:** 20 minutes (cap 25)
- **Audience:** adults and families (10+) who know a standard deck; solo players
- **Complexity:** 2 / 5

## Core mechanics and theme
- An engine-builder played with **only a standard 52-card deck** plus one printed rule card. Suits are resources, ranks are upgrade levels, and powers come from the structure of a familiar deck (suit, rank, pairs, runs) rather than custom card text.
- Theme direction: a clockmaker's workshop. Cards become gears, springs and finished clocks.
- Success means the game feels like a real engine-builder (a choice of what to build, compounding output), not a solitaire variant.

## Market gap
- Setup time is named as board gaming's top barrier to getting games played, and small-box, solo-capable games are the rising format (paperwave, zatu 2026 solo-boom piece).
- Standard-deck games are mostly solitaire, shedding or trick-taking. Engine-builders nearly always bind to custom card powers, so a standard-deck engine-builder is scarce.
- Demand scored 4, not 5: evidence is trend-level, from search summaries only. No pages were opened and no sales data exists.

## Comparables
1. **Wingspan Pocket** (BGG rating not found): a big publisher in the small-box engine format (July 2026, 1-5p, 25-35 min). This needs no custom cards at all, so setup takes seconds and the product is a rule sheet.
2. **Sprawlopolis** (rating not found): standard-style small-box puzzle with a custom 18-card set. This is a real engine with compounding output, using a deck anyone owns.
3. **Pocket Tableau idea (banked)**: also an engine tableau, but custom cards. Avoid overlapping with it.

## Rubric scores (27/30)
Demand 4, Gap 4, Originality 4, Producibility 5, Simulatability 5, Owner fit 5.

## Constraints for the designer
- Components: one 52-card standard deck (no jokers needed, or at most 2) and one printed rule card. No tokens beyond optional pencil-and-paper tallies.
- Rules fit one page, with at most 3 special rules. Set-up under 1 minute.
- Rank math must be balanced: no dominant strategy, every suit useful, zero dead cards (KPI).
- Solo mode needs a score ladder and win condition; multiplayer needs real interaction (shared market or contested cards), not parallel solitaire.
- Seat balance gap 5 points or less; strategic-vs-random gap 20 points or more; runaway leader rate 65% or less; at least 2 lead changes per game.
- Simulated length within 20% of 20 minutes (16-24).
- Main risk: it must not read as a solitaire variant; test this in the critique.
