# Brief: Heavenly Bodies v2 (working title; slug `heavenly-bodies-v2`)

Idea-bank id: none (owner-supplied, `games/heavenly-bodies-v2/idea.md`; not from a scan). Comparables check only, 2026-10-10.

## Target
- Players 2-4 (2 and 3 the core; drop any count that cannot be balanced). Play time 10-15 min. Ages 8+. Complexity 1.5/5. Teach in under 10 minutes, no hero abilities, no Star powers.
- Audience: casual to light-strategy card gamers, family and game-night filler.

## Core mechanics and theme
Each player has a small orbit (ring of 3-4 slots) of cards. Orbits **rotate** on a turn, cards **leave and enter orbit**, and **collisions change owners** (steal, swap, capture). Theme: planets, moons, comets in a shared sky. Cards should be constantly moving across the table.

## Market gap
- Orbital theme in small card games exists but is a quiet area: **Orbita** (25th Century Games, 2025, 2p, 15 min, about $15) uses planets on a track and a "win by the right margin" scoring; cards change hands only at the end. A BGG figure appears in snippets but conflicts (6.7 from 61 ratings vs 5.5; treat as `null`).
- **dnup** (Kei Kajino; Asmodee 2026, 2-5p, about 15 min, $15 MSRP) shows demand for a "rotate/flip your cards" hook in a 15-minute card game, praised for clever timing, but it is a climbing/shedding game with no orbit and no ownership change by collision.
- Take-that steal games (Frantic, Love Letter, Elimino) are common; light, fast, "card flies to the other side of the table" play is not tied to a spatial rotation mechanic. Skyjo/Cabo show fast, light, mostly low-conflict games sell.
- Gap: a kinetic, low-rules, multiplayer game where **position** (rotation) decides who captures what, with more than one way to win.
- Price and count norms: about $12-16, 2-5p, 15 min, 50-110 cards or fewer.

## Comparables and how v2 differs
1. **Orbita**: orbit track, 2p, margin scoring. v2: multiplayer, direct card capture and swaps, no margin-balancing.
2. **dnup**: flip/rotate hand for timing. v2: rotation applies to a visible table orbit, not a hand; interaction by collision.
3. **Love Letter / Frantic (take-that)**: steal and attack. v2: stealing is a consequence of position and timing, not a targeted card.

Avoid copying: Orbita's "win by exact margin" scoring, dnup's two-number flip-card and ladder-climbing, Scout-style take-and-insert. Avoid saturated areas: plain set collection and generic take-that attacks.

## Mechanic directions (designer picks one or blends two)
A. **Shared ring.** One central ring of 6 slots; a turn rotates it one step, then you play a card into the slot facing you; a card landing on an occupied slot bumps the old card to the bumped player's orbit.
B. **Personal orbits, passing rotation.** Each player has 3 slots; on a turn "rotate" moves all your cards one slot, and the card pushed off the end goes into the orbit of the player to your left (cards travel round the table).
C. **Collision duel.** Cards have Size; when a rotation or play puts two cards in a slot, the larger captures the smaller; the loser goes to the winner's capture pile or is exiled to Out of Orbit.
D. **Escape velocity.** Moving a card out of orbit (launch) is a risky action to a shared Deep Space pool that anyone may claim with a matching card; moving in (capture) costs a card from your hand.
E. **Gravity swap.** Playing a card swaps it with the card in the same slot of any opponent's orbit; rotation then shifts everyone's positions so swaps threaten different targets each turn.

## Candidate win conditions (use 2 or 3 at once)
1. **Constellation:** hold three cards of one matching type (or chain of sizes) in your orbit at the end of your turn.
2. **Critical Mass:** orbit total Size at least N and keep it through a full round.
3. **Collector or Eclipse:** first to capture a set number of cards, or to empty your hand; rotation can also "eclipse" an opponent, ending the game on a full set held by none.

Comeback mechanism needed (design-rules 1): swaps and captures should let trailers take from leaders; state the knob.
