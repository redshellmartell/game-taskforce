# Split the Take - Rules (v2, revision 1)

## 1. Overview

- **Title:** Split the Take
- **Hook:** A trick-taking heist for exactly three. Each round two of you are the crew and must land the haul the Planner promised: exactly on the Target is a clean job, one off is a messy job, and two or more off means the job is blown. The third player is the Double-Crosser, who gets paid when the job is blown, by grabbing tricks or by slipping the crew too many.
- **Players:** exactly 3
- **Play time:** about 20 minutes (6 rounds); short game 10 minutes (3 rounds)
- **Age:** 10+
- **Complexity:** 2 / 5 (assumes players know "follow suit" and trumps)

## 2. Components

40 standard-size cards and a score pad.

**Loot cards (36):** 4 suits x ranks 1-9. Each card appears once.

| Suit | Ranks | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Coins | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Gems | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Keys | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Masks | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |

Loot cards have no point values; only the number of tricks won matters.

**Role cards (3):**

| Card | Count | Text on card |
|---|---|---|
| Planner | 1 | "Name the trump suit and the Target: 3 to 7. Clean job +3. Messy job +1." |
| Safecracker | 1 | "Before play: draw 3 from the stash, then discard 3. Clean job +4. Messy job +2." |
| Double-Crosser | 1 | "Lead the first trick. Messy job +1. Blown job +5." |

**Heat card (1):** "Your bonus this round is 2 less (not below 0)."

**Score pad** (not a card): one column per player, one row per round.

Total: 36 + 3 + 1 = 40 cards.

## 3. Setup

1. Seat the 3 players around the table.
2. Shuffle the 3 role cards and deal one face up to each player. Whoever gets Planner is the first Planner. Then rearrange the other two role cards so that the player on the Planner's **left** holds the Safecracker and the player on the Planner's **right** holds the Double-Crosser.
3. Choose the game length: **6 rounds** (standard; each player is each role twice) or **3 rounds** (short; each player is each role once). Only these two lengths are allowed so roles stay equal.
4. Put the Heat card to one side. Write each player's name on the score pad; all scores start at 0.

## 4. Turn structure

The game is played in rounds. Each round has these phases, in order.

### Phase 1 - Heat
If one player has a strictly higher total score than both others, that player takes the Heat card for this round. Otherwise (round 1, or a tie for the highest score) nobody has Heat. Return the Heat card at the end of the round.

### Phase 2 - Deal
The Double-Crosser shuffles all 36 loot cards and deals **7 cards face down to each player**, one at a time, starting with the player on the Double-Crosser's left (the Planner) and going clockwise. The remaining 15 cards form the **stash**, face down. Nobody may look at the stash.

### Phase 3 - The Plan (Planner)
The Planner looks at their hand and announces, out loud:
- **Trump:** one of Coins, Gems, Keys or Masks. (There is no No Trump option.)
- **Target:** a whole number from **3 to 7**. This is the number of tricks the crew (Planner + Safecracker, added together) is aiming to win this round.

Both choices are final for the round. Record the trump and Target on the score pad.

### Phase 4 - The Swap (Safecracker)
The Safecracker takes the top **3** cards of the stash into their hand (now 10 cards), then discards exactly **3** cards from their hand face down (they may discard any of the cards they just drew). Discards are out of the round and nobody may look at them. The Safecracker now has 7 cards again. The swap is mandatory.

### Phase 5 - Tricks
Play 7 tricks.
1. The **Double-Crosser leads the first trick**. After that, the winner of each trick leads the next.
2. The leader plays any one card face up. Then the other two players each play one card, in clockwise order.
3. **Follow suit:** a player who has a card of the led suit must play one. A player with no card of the led suit may play any card (including a trump).
4. **Winning a trick:** if any trumps were played, the highest trump wins. Otherwise the highest card of the led suit wins. (Every card is unique, so there are never ties.)
5. The winner keeps the trick face down in front of them. Each trick won is one **loot**.

### Phase 6 - Scoring
Count the tricks won by each player. Crew tricks = Planner's tricks + Safecracker's tricks. Compare crew tricks with the Target:
- **Clean job:** crew tricks equal the Target exactly.
- **Messy job:** crew tricks are exactly 1 more or exactly 1 less than the Target.
- **Blown job:** crew tricks differ from the Target by 2 or more.

| Who | Loot | Clean job | Messy job | Blown job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Planner | +1 per trick won | +3 | +1 | 0 |
| Safecracker | +1 per trick won | +4 | +2 | 0 |
| Double-Crosser | +1 per trick won | 0 | +1 | +5 |

The numbers in the last three columns are **bonuses**. Every player always scores loot.

**Heat:** the player holding the Heat card subtracts 2 from their bonus this round. A bonus can never go below 0, so Heat on a bonus of +1 leaves 0, and Heat on a player whose bonus is 0 has no effect. Heat never affects loot points.

Write all scores on the pad at the same time.

### Phase 7 - Rotate
Each player passes their role card to the player on their **right**. As a result the Planner becomes the Safecracker, the Safecracker becomes the Double-Crosser, and the Double-Crosser becomes the Planner. (The Safecracker is always on the new Planner's left, and the Double-Crosser on the new Planner's right.) Start the next round.

### Every legal action, summarised
| Phase | Who | Legal choices |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Planner | Trump: 4 options (Coins, Gems, Keys, Masks). Target: 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7. |
| Swap | Safecracker | Which 3 of their 10 cards to discard (120 possible sets). |
| Tricks | Leader of a trick | Any card in hand. |
| Tricks | Follower | Any card of the led suit if they have one; otherwise any card. |

There are no other actions. Players may not pass, show their hands or make binding deals. Players may not say anything about the cards in their hand; comments about scores and the Target are allowed.

## 5. Special rules and timing

The game has exactly three special rules:
1. **Clean / messy / blown job** - the crew's bonus depends on how close crew tricks land to the Target; the Double-Crosser is paid mainly when the job is blown.
2. **The Swap** - the Safecracker's mandatory draw-3, discard-3 after the Plan.
3. **Heat** - the sole score leader's bonus is 2 lower this round (not below 0).

Timing and edge cases:
- **Order within a round:** Heat, Deal, Plan, Swap, Tricks, Scoring, Rotate. Heat is decided from totals *before* the round's deal and does not change during the round.
- **Simultaneous scoring:** all Phase 6 points are added at once, after the last trick. Nothing scores during tricks.
- **Ties for the lead (Heat):** if two or three players share the highest total, nobody gets Heat.
- **Empty stash:** cannot happen (the stash has 15 cards and only 3 are drawn per round).
- **Unreachable results:** all Targets 3-7 are legal with any trump. Crew tricks always range from 0 to 7, so every round is exactly one of clean, messy or blown.
- **Misplay (revoke):** if a player fails to follow suit when able and it is noticed before the round's scoring, play is not replayed. The offender scores 0 for the round (no loot, no bonus). The other two players score normally, using the tricks exactly as played (including the offender's tricks when counting crew tricks). In simulation, revokes are impossible.
- **Crew conflict:** the Planner and Safecracker both gain from the job but compete for loot. They may not discuss their hands.

## 6. End of game and scoring

- **End trigger:** the game ends after the Scoring phase of round 6 (standard) or round 3 (short game). There is no other end condition.
- **Winner:** the player with the highest total score.
- **Tiebreaker 1:** among tied players, the one with more **blown jobs as Double-Crosser** (rounds in which they were Double-Crosser and crew tricks differed from the Target by 2 or more). These count whether or not Heat reduced the bonus.
- **Tiebreaker 2:** the tied player who won more tricks over the whole game.
- **If still tied:** the tied players share the win.

Record per round on the pad: trump, Target, each player's tricks, the result (clean, messy or blown), each player's bonus, and who held Heat. This keeps the tiebreakers checkable.

## 7. Design notes

**The twist: a shared contract with a two-sided spoiler.** The crew shares one contract; the Double-Crosser wrecks it either by grabbing tricks (crew falls short) or by ducking and dumping high cards so the crew is forced to win too many. In v2 the Double-Crosser must push the count **two** away from the Target to get paid in full, so a lazy "cash my top cards" round no longer earns +5; they have to read where the count is heading and commit to one direction. The crew, in turn, has a real reason to steer: clean pays much more than messy, and messy still pays something, so the Target is a promise worth keeping rather than a coin flip.

**Why the bands.** In v1 an exact hit happened 19.7% of the time, so the Double-Crosser was paid in 80% of rounds and plain trick-grabbing won. With a 3-wide "job comes off" band (Target - 1 to Target + 1) the crew should be paid in roughly half of rounds or more, and the extra 2 points for an exact landing keeps the end of each round tense ("we're on 4 of 5: do I take this trick or duck it?").

**Each role has one distinct power and one distinct tension.**
- *Planner* chooses trump and Target with no knowledge of the Safecracker's swap. A Target set too high or too low hands the Double-Crosser +5.
- *Safecracker* knows the plan before swapping, takes the bigger cut of a successful job, and has a stronger swap (3 cards). They decide whether to build trump strength, void a suit to ruff, or shed winners that would overshoot. They play last to the first trick.
- *Double-Crosser* leads first and earns +5 for a blown job, +1 even for a messy one (so they always want the count pushed away from the Target).
- The crew are partners for the job but rivals for loot.

**Strategic play should now beat greedy play.** Bonuses (up to 4 or 5) are worth more than the 2-3 tricks a player usually takes, and they are reachable. A crew member who grabs every trick risks a messy or blown job (losing 2-4 bonus points) for 1 loot point; a Double-Crosser who grabs every trick against a low Target pushes the crew *toward* it.

**Anti-kingmaking.** The contract is shared: the Double-Crosser cannot make one crew member succeed and the other fail. Their bonus decision ("push the count up, push it down, or give up") hurts both rivals in the same way. The only lopsided effect they have is on how loot is spread between the crew (at most a few points).

**Catch-up and tension.** Heat now costs the sole leader 2 bonus points (about two-thirds of a typical bonus), so a leader who wants to stay ahead must out-loot or out-plan the field, while a trailing player is never stopped from scoring. Bonuses range from 0 to 5 and swing against loot of 0-7, so one round can change the lead. Rough expectation: 9-11 points enter the game per round; final scores near 20-30.

**Seat balance.** Roles rotate every round and only 3- or 6-round games are allowed, so every seat plays each role equally often. The seat that plans in the last round has a small information edge about the standings.

**Length.** 7 tricks per round at about 15-20 seconds each, plus about 1 minute for dealing, planning, swapping and scoring, gives about 3-3.5 minutes per round, so 6 rounds is about 18-21 minutes.

**What the playtester should check first.** (1) Job comes off (clean or messy) 40-60%+, clean alone 20-35%. (2) Mixed table: strategic at or above greedy. (3) Lead changes >= 2 and sole leader after round 3 wins <= 65%. (4) Points per round: Safecracker and Double-Crosser within about 1 point. (5) Seat gap <= 5 on 4,000+ games. If the job comes off much more than 65%, cut the messy bonuses to Planner 0 / Safecracker +1; if much less than 40%, widen the Target range to 2-7.

**Bot hints for simulation.**
- *Planner:* estimate crew tricks as 2 x (own trumps of rank 6+, plus off-suit 8s and 9s, plus 1 if holding 4+ trumps), clamp to 3-7, and set that as the Target. Trump = the suit with most cards (ties: higher total rank).
- *Safecracker:* keep high trumps; discard to make a void in a short non-trump suit; if the Planner's Target is low (3-4), shed off-suit 9s and 8s instead.
- *Crew in play:* track crew tricks. Once crew tricks equal the Target, try to lose every remaining trick (play the lowest legal card, so that the Double-Crosser wins the trick where possible). Before that, play to win when a cheap winner exists.
- *Double-Crosser:* estimate the crew's final count from tricks so far plus the crew's likely winners. If the estimate is at or below the Target, win tricks (push it down); if above, duck and dump high cards (push it up). Re-evaluate each trick.
- *Greedy bot (comparison):* Target = estimate as above; always win a trick if able with the lowest winning card, otherwise play the lowest card.

## 8. Changelog

**v2 (revision 1, 2026-10-04).** Fixes from playtest-report.md (NEEDS-FIXES), critique.md (REVISE-MAJOR) and panel.json. panel.json at revision 0 contains no Bar Raiser entry and no active veto flags; the panel's repeated pet peeves (rulebook weight, runaway leaders, ambiguity) are addressed by changes 4-6.

1. **Exact hit replaced by clean / messy / blown bands.** Clean (exact) pays the crew, messy (off by 1) pays the crew a smaller amount and the Double-Crosser +1, blown (off by 2+) pays the Double-Crosser +5. *Why:* playtest problem 1 and critic required change 1 - exact hits were 19.7%, so the DC's +4 was nearly free. *KPI:* contract success (clean or messy) from 19.7% to 40-60%+; DC points per round from 4.8 down to within about 1 point of the other roles.
2. **Bonuses enlarged relative to loot, and a payout for landing exactly.** Clean +3/+4 vs messy +1/+2 means a correctly steered round is worth 2 more points per crew member than a nearly-steered one, and an overshoot or undershoot of 2 costs everything. *Why:* playtest problem 2 - greedy beat strategic 68% to 27% because the contract almost never paid, so only loot mattered. *KPI:* mixed table strategic win rate at or above greedy (target: strategic >= 40%, greedy <= 40%).
3. **Safecracker strengthened: draws and discards 3 (was 2) and takes the bigger cut (+4 clean, +2 messy).** *Why:* playtest problem 3 and critic required change 2 - Safecracker earned 2.7 points per round vs DC 4.8 (win correlation -0.52). *KPI:* Safecracker vs DC points per round within about 1; Safecracker "dominated" flag clears.
4. **Heat now -2 (not below 0), and it is defined when the bonus is 0.** *Why:* playtest problem 4 and critic required change 3 - Heat at -1 cost the leader only about 0.45 points and removing it barely changed anything. Together with larger, more volatile bonuses (change 1) this should create more swings. *KPI:* lead changes from 1.27 to 2 or more; sole-leader-after-round-3 win rate from 64.4% to 60% or less.
5. **No Trump removed.** *Why:* playtest problem 5 and critic required change 4 - chosen 0% of the time; cutting it also shortens the rules. *KPI:* zero dead options; panel rules simplicity up from 0.41.
6. **Rule ambiguities fixed.** Revoke now has one meaning (offender scores 0; contract counted as played). Heat on a 0 bonus has no effect. The role pass is "pass to your right", with the resulting roles spelled out. Tiebreaker 1 counts blown jobs as DC whether or not Heat applied. The deal order is stated. Table talk is limited to "nothing about your hand". *Why:* playtest ambiguities list and critic required change 5. *KPI:* zero rule ambiguities.
7. **Seat balance and kingmaking kept in check without structural changes.** Rotation and 3/6-round lengths are unchanged; every bonus the DC can cause or deny still hits both crew members in the same direction, and the new messy +1 for the DC is paid on the same condition as the crew's messy bonus. *Why:* brief constraints (seat gap <= 5, spoiler must not kingmake) and critic required change 6. *KPI:* seat gap <= 5 points on a 4,000+ game run.
