# Split the Take - Rules (v1)

## 1. Overview

- **Title:** Split the Take
- **Hook:** A trick-taking heist for exactly three. Each round two of you are the crew, and you must take *exactly* the number of tricks the Planner promised. The third player is the Double-Crosser, who wrecks the job by taking too many tricks or by slipping you too many.
- **Players:** exactly 3
- **Play time:** about 20 minutes (6 rounds); short game 10 minutes (3 rounds)
- **Age:** 10+
- **Complexity:** 2 / 5 (assumes players know "follow suit" and trumps)

## 2. Components

40 standard-size cards and a score pad.

**Loot cards (36):** 4 suits x ranks 1-9. Each card appears once.

| Suit | Ranks | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Coins | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Gems | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Keys | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Masks | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |

Loot cards have no point values; only the number of tricks won matters.

**Role cards (3):**

| Card | Count | Text on card |
|---|---|---|
| Planner | 1 | "Name the trump suit (or No Trump) and the Target: 3 to 7. Crew bonus +3 if the crew takes exactly the Target." |
| Safecracker | 1 | "Before play: draw 2 from the stash, then discard 2. Crew bonus +3 if the crew takes exactly the Target." |
| Double-Crosser | 1 | "Lead the first trick. +4 if the crew misses the Target." |

**Heat card (1):** "Your bonus this round is 1 less."

**Score pad** (not a card): one column per player, one row per round.

Total: 36 + 3 + 1 = 40 cards.

## 3. Setup

1. Seat the 3 players around the table. Play always passes clockwise (to the left).
2. Pick a starting Planner at random (for example, shuffle the 3 role cards and deal one face up to each player; whoever gets Planner starts as Planner). Then give the Safecracker card to the player on the Planner's left and the Double-Crosser card to the player on the Planner's right.
3. Choose the game length: **6 rounds** (standard; each player is each role twice) or **3 rounds** (short; each player is each role once). Only these two lengths are allowed so roles stay equal.
4. Put the Heat card to one side. Write each player's name on the score pad; all scores start at 0.

## 4. Turn structure

The game is played in rounds. Each round has these phases, in order.

### Phase 1 - Heat
If one player has a strictly higher total score than both others, that player takes the Heat card for this round. Otherwise (round 1, or a tie for the highest score) nobody has Heat. Return the Heat card at the end of the round.

### Phase 2 - Deal
The Double-Crosser shuffles all 36 loot cards and deals **7 cards face down to each player**, one at a time, starting with the Planner. The remaining 15 cards form the **stash**, face down. Nobody may look at the stash.

### Phase 3 - The Plan (Planner)
The Planner looks at their hand and announces, out loud and at the same time:
- **Trump:** one of Coins, Gems, Keys, Masks, or **No Trump**.
- **Target:** a whole number from **3 to 7**. This is the number of tricks the crew (Planner + Safecracker, added together) must win this round.

Both choices are final for the round. Record the Target on the score pad.

### Phase 4 - The Swap (Safecracker)
The Safecracker takes the top 2 cards of the stash into their hand (now 9 cards), then discards exactly 2 cards from their hand face down (they may discard the cards they just drew). Discards are out of the round and nobody may look at them. The Safecracker now has 7 cards again. The swap is mandatory.

### Phase 5 - Tricks
Play 7 tricks.
1. The **Double-Crosser leads the first trick**. After that, the winner of each trick leads the next.
2. The leader plays any one card face up. Then the other two players each play one card, in clockwise order.
3. **Follow suit:** a player who has a card of the led suit must play one. A player with no card of the led suit may play any card (including a trump).
4. **Winning a trick:** if any trumps were played, the highest trump wins. Otherwise the highest card of the led suit wins. Under No Trump, the highest card of the led suit always wins. (Every card is unique, so there are never ties.)
5. The winner keeps the trick face down in front of them. Each trick won is one **loot**.

### Phase 6 - Scoring
Count the tricks won by each player. Crew tricks = Planner's tricks + Safecracker's tricks.

| Who | Scores |
|---|---|
| Every player | **+1 per trick they won** (loot) |
| Planner and Safecracker | **+3 each** if crew tricks **exactly equal** the Target (the job comes off) |
| Double-Crosser | **+4** if crew tricks are **more or less** than the Target (double-cross) |

**Heat:** the player holding the Heat card reduces their bonus by 1 (crew bonus +3 becomes +2; double-cross bonus +4 becomes +3). Heat never affects loot points.

Write all scores on the pad at the same time.

### Phase 7 - Rotate
Pass each role card one seat to the left: the Planner becomes the Safecracker, the Safecracker becomes the Double-Crosser, and the Double-Crosser becomes the Planner. Start the next round.

### Every legal action, summarised
| Phase | Who | Legal choices |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Planner | Trump: 5 options (4 suits or No Trump). Target: 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7. |
| Swap | Safecracker | Which 2 of their 9 cards to discard (36 possible pairs). |
| Tricks | Leader of a trick | Any card in hand. |
| Tricks | Follower | Any card of the led suit if they have one; otherwise any card. |

There are no other actions. Players may not pass, show their hands or make binding deals. Table talk about the cards in hand is not allowed.

## 5. Special rules and timing

The game has exactly three special rules:
1. **Exact job / double-cross** - the crew scores its bonus only on an exact hit; the Double-Crosser scores when the crew is over *or* under.
2. **The Swap** - the Safecracker's mandatory draw-2, discard-2 after the Plan.
3. **Heat** - the sole score leader's bonus is 1 lower this round.

Timing and edge cases:
- **Order within a round:** Heat, Deal, Plan, Swap, Tricks, Scoring, Rotate. Heat is decided from totals *before* the round's deal and does not change during the round.
- **Simultaneous scoring:** all Phase 6 points are added at once, after the last trick. Nothing scores during tricks.
- **Ties for the lead (Heat):** if two or three players share the highest total, nobody gets Heat.
- **Empty stash:** cannot happen (the stash has 15 cards and only 2 are drawn per round).
- **Misplay (revoke):** if a player fails to follow suit when able and it is noticed before the round's scoring, the round is scored as if the offender won 0 tricks and the bonus they could earn is lost; the other players score normally, with the trick count for the contract taken as played. In simulation, revokes are impossible.
- **Crew conflict:** the Planner and Safecracker share the bonus but compete for loot. They may not discuss their hands.
- **Target of 7 with No Trump, etc.:** all combinations of trump and Target are legal.

## 6. End of game and scoring

- **End trigger:** the game ends after the Scoring phase of round 6 (standard) or round 3 (short game). There is no other end condition.
- **Winner:** the player with the highest total score.
- **Tiebreaker 1:** among tied players, the one with more successful double-crosses (rounds in which they were Double-Crosser and the crew missed the Target).
- **Tiebreaker 2:** the tied player who won more tricks over the whole game.
- **If still tied:** the tied players share the win.

Record per round on the pad: Target, each player's tricks, each player's bonus, and who held Heat. This keeps the tiebreakers checkable.

## 7. Design notes

**The twist: exact two-sided denial.** In most trick-takers the defender stops a contract by winning tricks. Here the crew must hit the Target *exactly*, so the Double-Crosser can wreck the job in two ways: by grabbing tricks (crew falls short) or by ducking and dumping high cards so the crew is forced to win too many. Every Double-Crosser play asks "do I need to win this trick, or make sure *they* win it?" Ducking costs them loot points, so denial is never free.

**Each role has one distinct power and one distinct tension.**
- *Planner* chooses trump and Target with no knowledge of the Safecracker's swap. High Targets mean more loot for the crew but are easier to push over or under. Low Targets hand loot to the Double-Crosser.
- *Safecracker* knows the plan before swapping, so the swap is a precise decision: build toward the trump suit, create a void to ruff, or dump high cards that would push the crew over. Last to act in the first trick.
- *Double-Crosser* leads first and only needs the count to be off by one in either direction.
- The crew are partners for the bonus but rivals for loot, so the Safecracker is always weighing "take this trick myself" against "let the Planner have it".

**Anti-kingmaking.** The contract is shared: the Double-Crosser cannot make one crew member succeed and the other fail. Their only bonus decision is "break it or not", which hurts both rivals equally. The only lopsided effect they have is on how loot is spread between the crew (at most a few points).

**Catch-up and tension.** Heat takes 1 point from the sole leader's bonus every round they lead, so a leader must keep winning by loot as well as bonuses. Bonuses (3-4) are close to a round's loot (7 tricks spread over 3 players), so one round can swing the lead. Rough expectation: about 11-12 points enter the game per round, so final scores land near 20-25 in a 6-round game with frequent lead changes.

**Seat balance.** Roles rotate left every round, and only 3- or 6-round games are allowed, so every seat plays each role equally often. The seat that plans in the last round has a small information edge about the standings; Heat and the fixed rotation are meant to keep the seat gap at 5 points or less.

**Length.** 7 tricks per round at about 15-20 seconds each, plus about 1 minute for dealing, planning, swapping and scoring, gives about 3-3.5 minutes per round, so 6 rounds is about 18-21 minutes.

**What the playtester should check first.** (1) Seat balance gap. (2) Contract success rate by Target (aim for roughly 40-60% overall; if far outside, adjust the bonuses 3/4 or the Target range). (3) Whether a fixed Target (for example always 5) is dominant; if so, consider scoring the crew bonus as Target - 2. (4) Whether the Double-Crosser ducking strategy is ever correct in bot play.

**Bot hints for simulation.** A reasonable heuristic Planner counts expected winners (trumps of rank 6+, off-suit 8s and 9s) and doubles for the crew, clamped to 3-7, choosing the trump suit where it holds the most cards (ties: higher total rank). A heuristic Double-Crosser compares crew tricks so far plus likely remaining winners with the Target: if the crew is on track to fall short, win tricks; if on track to exceed it, duck; if on track to hit it exactly, win tricks (the cheaper direction, since winning also earns loot).
