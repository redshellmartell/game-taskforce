# Split the Take - Rules (v3, revision 2)

## 1. Overview

- **Title:** Split the Take
- **Hook:** A trick-taking heist for exactly three. Each round two of you are the crew and must land the haul the Planner promised: exactly on the Target is a clean job, one off is a messy job, and two or more off means the job is blown. A blown job means the crew walks away with nothing, not even the loot they grabbed. The third player is the Double-Crosser, who always keeps their own loot and gets paid when the job is blown, by grabbing tricks or by slipping the crew too many.
- **Players:** exactly 3
- **Play time:** about 20 minutes (6 rounds); short game 10 minutes (3 rounds)
- **Age:** 10+
- **Complexity:** 2 / 5 (assumes players know "follow suit" and trumps)

## 2. Components

40 standard-size cards and a score pad.

**Loot cards (36):** 4 suits x ranks 1-9. Each card appears once.

| Suit | Ranks | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Coins | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Gems | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Keys | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |
| Masks | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 | 9 |

Loot cards have no point values; only the number of tricks won matters.

**Role cards (3):**

| Card | Count | Text on card |
|---|---|---|
| Planner | 1 | "Name the trump suit and the Target: 3 to 7. Clean job +5. Messy job +2. Blown job: you score nothing." |
| Safecracker | 1 | "Before play: draw 3 from the stash, then discard 3. Clean job +6. Messy job +2. Blown job: you score nothing." |
| Double-Crosser | 1 | "Lead the first trick. Always score your loot. Blown job +3." |

**Heat card (1):** "Your bonus this round is 3 less (not below 0)."

**Score pad** (not a card): one column per player, one row per round.

Total: 36 + 3 + 1 = 40 cards.

## 3. Setup

1. Seat the 3 players around the table.
2. Shuffle the 3 role cards and deal one face up to each player. Whoever gets Planner is the first Planner. Then rearrange the other two role cards so that the player on the Planner's **left** holds the Safecracker and the player on the Planner's **right** holds the Double-Crosser.
3. Choose the game length: **6 rounds** (standard; each player is each role twice) or **3 rounds** (short; each player is each role once). Only these two lengths are allowed so roles stay equal.
4. Put the Heat card to one side. Write each player's name on the score pad; all scores start at 0.

## 4. Turn structure

The game is played in rounds. Each round has these phases, in order.

### Phase 1 - Heat
If one player has a strictly higher total score than both others, that player takes the Heat card for this round. Otherwise (round 1, or a tie for the highest score) nobody has Heat. Return the Heat card at the end of the round.

### Phase 2 - Deal
The Double-Crosser shuffles all 36 loot cards and deals **7 cards face down to each player**, one at a time, starting with the player on the Double-Crosser's left (the Planner) and going clockwise. The remaining 15 cards form the **stash**, face down. Nobody may look at the stash.

### Phase 3 - The Plan (Planner)
The Planner looks at their hand and announces, out loud:
- **Trump:** one of Coins, Gems, Keys or Masks. (There is no No Trump option.)
- **Target:** a whole number from **3 to 7**. This is the number of tricks the crew (Planner + Safecracker, added together) is aiming to win this round.

Both choices are final for the round. Record the trump and Target on the score pad.

### Phase 4 - The Swap (Safecracker)
The Safecracker takes the top **3** cards of the stash into their hand (now 10 cards), then discards exactly **3** cards from their hand face down (they may discard any of the cards they just drew). Discards are out of the round and nobody may look at them. The Safecracker now has 7 cards again. The swap is mandatory.

### Phase 5 - Tricks
Play 7 tricks.
1. The **Double-Crosser leads the first trick**. After that, the winner of each trick leads the next.
2. The leader plays any one card face up. Then the other two players each play one card, in clockwise order.
3. **Follow suit:** a player who has a card of the led suit must play one. A player with no card of the led suit may play any card (including a trump).
4. **Winning a trick:** if any trumps were played, the highest trump wins. Otherwise the highest card of the led suit wins. (Every card is unique, so there are never ties.)
5. The winner keeps the trick face down in front of them. Each trick won is one **loot**.

### Phase 6 - Scoring
Count the tricks won by each player. Crew tricks = Planner's tricks + Safecracker's tricks. Compare crew tricks with the Target:
- **Clean job:** crew tricks equal the Target exactly.
- **Messy job:** crew tricks are exactly 1 more or exactly 1 less than the Target.
- **Blown job:** crew tricks differ from the Target by 2 or more.

The job **comes off** if it is clean or messy.

| Who | Loot (1 point per trick won) | Clean job bonus | Messy job bonus | Blown job bonus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Planner | only if the job comes off | +5 | +2 | 0 (and no loot) |
| Safecracker | only if the job comes off | +6 | +2 | 0 (and no loot) |
| Double-Crosser | always | 0 | 0 | +3 |

In words:
- **Crew (Planner and Safecracker):** if the job comes off, each scores 1 point per trick they personally won, plus their bonus. If the job is blown, each scores 0 for the round.
- **Double-Crosser:** always scores 1 point per trick they won, plus 3 if the job is blown.

**Heat:** the player holding the Heat card subtracts 3 from their bonus this round. A bonus can never go below 0, so Heat on a messy +2 leaves 0, Heat on a clean +5 leaves 2, and Heat on a player whose bonus is 0 has no effect. Heat never affects loot points.

Write all scores on the pad at the same time.

### Phase 7 - Rotate
Each player passes their role card to the player on their **right**. As a result the Planner becomes the Safecracker, the Safecracker becomes the Double-Crosser, and the Double-Crosser becomes the Planner. (The Safecracker is always on the new Planner's left, and the Double-Crosser on the new Planner's right.) Start the next round.

### Every legal action, summarised
| Phase | Who | Legal choices |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Planner | Trump: 4 options (Coins, Gems, Keys, Masks). Target: 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7. |
| Swap | Safecracker | Which 3 of their 10 cards to discard (120 possible sets). |
| Tricks | Leader of a trick | Any card in hand. |
| Tricks | Follower | Any card of the led suit if they have one; otherwise any card. |

There are no other actions. Players may not pass, show their hands or make binding deals. Players may not say anything about the cards in their hand; comments about scores and the Target are allowed.

## 5. Special rules and timing

The game has exactly three special rules:
1. **Clean / messy / blown job** - the crew is paid (loot and bonus) only if the job comes off, and paid most for landing exactly; the Double-Crosser is paid extra when the job is blown.
2. **The Swap** - the Safecracker's mandatory draw-3, discard-3 after the Plan.
3. **Heat** - the sole score leader's bonus is 3 lower this round (not below 0).

Timing and edge cases:
- **Order within a round:** Heat, Deal, Plan, Swap, Tricks, Scoring, Rotate. Heat is decided from totals *before* the round's deal and does not change during the round.
- **Simultaneous scoring:** all Phase 6 points are added at once, after the last trick. Nothing scores during tricks.
- **Ties for the lead (Heat):** if two or three players share the highest total, nobody gets Heat.
- **Empty stash:** cannot happen (the stash has 15 cards and only 3 are drawn per round).
- **Unreachable results:** all Targets 3-7 are legal with any trump. Crew tricks always range from 0 to 7, so every round is exactly one of clean, messy or blown.
- **Misplay (revoke):** if a player fails to follow suit when able and it is noticed before the round's scoring, play is not replayed. The offender scores 0 for the round (no loot, no bonus). The other two players score normally, using the tricks exactly as played (including the offender's tricks when counting crew tricks). In simulation, revokes are impossible.
- **Crew conflict:** the Planner and Safecracker both need the job to come off but compete for loot within it. They may not discuss their hands.
- **Negative scores:** impossible. Every round's score for every player is 0 or more.

## 6. End of game and scoring

- **End trigger:** the game ends after the Scoring phase of round 6 (standard) or round 3 (short game). There is no other end condition.
- **Winner:** the player with the highest total score.
- **Tiebreaker 1:** among tied players, the one with more **blown jobs as Double-Crosser** (rounds in which they were Double-Crosser and crew tricks differed from the Target by 2 or more). These count whether or not Heat reduced the bonus.
- **Tiebreaker 2:** the tied player who won more tricks over the whole game (all tricks count, including tricks that scored 0 because a job was blown).
- **If still tied:** the tied players share the win.

Record per round on the pad: trump, Target, each player's tricks, the result (clean, messy or blown), each player's points, and who held Heat. This keeps the tiebreakers checkable.

## 7. Design notes

**The twist: a shared contract with a two-sided spoiler.** The crew shares one contract; the Double-Crosser wrecks it either by grabbing tricks (crew falls short) or by ducking and dumping high cards so the crew is forced to win too many. In v3 the stakes of that contract are total: a blown job wipes out the crew's loot as well as their bonus. Every trick a crew member takes past the safe band is not a free point, it is a bet with the whole round's score.

**Why v3 changes the loot rule rather than just the bonus size.** The revision 1 playtest showed that a crew member who grabbed every trick scored about 1 point more per round than one who steered, with the same contract success (about 50-55%), and that doubling or tripling bonuses only made greedy worse without making steering better. The diagnosis: overshooting was free, because loot was banked no matter what. Now a greedy crew member who drives crew tricks two past the Target loses everything they grabbed, while a crew member who ducks once the count is in the band keeps both loot and bonus. The Double-Crosser keeps their loot always, so for them grabbing is still a real way to play (it pushes the count down), which keeps their role simple.

**The crew's live decision each trick.** "We're on 4 of a Target of 4 with three tricks left: if I win this, we're messy (still paid, but -3 or -4 bonus); if we win two more, we're blown and I lose my 3 loot too." Before the Target is reached, each crew member still prefers to be the one who wins (loot is personal), so the crew are partners on the count and rivals on who takes each trick.

**Each role has one distinct power and one distinct tension.**
- *Planner* chooses trump and Target with no knowledge of the Safecracker's swap. A Target set too high or too low now costs the Planner their loot as well as their bonus.
- *Safecracker* knows the plan before swapping, takes the bigger clean bonus (+6), and has a strong swap (3 cards). They decide whether to build trump strength, void a suit to ruff, or shed winners that would overshoot. They play last to the first trick.
- *Double-Crosser* leads first, always keeps their loot, and earns +3 for a blown job. Blowing the job costs each crew member their whole round, so the swing between the DC and the crew on a blown job is large.

**Strategic play should now beat greedy play.** For a crew member, a clean job with 2 tricks is worth 7-8 points, a messy one 4, a blown one 0, whatever they grabbed. A greedy crew member who takes 4 tricks of a Target of 3 alongside a partner's 1 is messy (4 + 2 = 6); one more trick and they score 0. The expected cost of over-grabbing is now larger than the loot gained.

**Anti-kingmaking.** The contract is shared: the Double-Crosser cannot make one crew member succeed and the other fail. Blowing the job zeroes both crew members at once; the crew member who had grabbed more loot loses more, but that is a result of their own grabbing, not a choice the DC can aim at one player.

**Catch-up and tension.** Heat now costs the sole leader 3 bonus points (a messy bonus disappears entirely, a clean one drops to 2 or 3). Rounds are also more volatile than in v2, because a blown job takes the crew from 6-8 points to 0 in one trick. Both are meant to produce more lead changes, while a trailing player is never stopped from scoring. Rough expectation: 9-11 points enter the game per round; final scores near 18-28.

**Seat balance.** Roles rotate every round and only 3- or 6-round games are allowed, so every seat plays each role equally often. The seat that plans in the last round has a small information edge about the standings.

**Length.** 7 tricks per round at about 15-20 seconds each, plus about 1 minute for dealing, planning, swapping and scoring, gives about 3-3.5 minutes per round, so 6 rounds is about 18-21 minutes.

**Expected role points (from v2 result rates, before steering improves).** With clean about 25%, messy about 32%, blown about 43%, and about 2.25 tricks per crew member: Planner about 3.2, Safecracker about 3.4, Double-Crosser about 3.8 points per round. Better steering raises clean and lowers blown, which moves points from the DC to the crew, so roles should converge. If the DC ends up more than 1 point per round above the crew, cut the blown bonus to +2; if more than 1 below, raise it to +4.

**What the playtester should check first.** (1) Mixed table: strategic at or above greedy and at least 40%. (2) Lead changes >= 2 and sole leader after round 3 wins <= 65%. (3) Job comes off 40-60%+, clean alone 20-35%. (4) Role points per round within about 1. (5) Seat gap <= 5 on 4,000+ games. Suggested experiments if a KPI misses: (a) the critic's flat version, everyone scores 1 point per 2 tricks (rounded down), clean +5/+6, messy +2, blown DC +3, no conditional loot; (b) crew loot only on a clean job; (c) final round scored double, for lead changes.

**Bot hints for simulation.**
- *Planner:* estimate crew tricks as 2 x (own trumps of rank 6+, plus off-suit 8s and 9s, plus 1 if holding 4+ trumps), clamp to 3-7, and set that as the Target. Trump = the suit with most cards (ties: higher total rank).
- *Safecracker:* keep high trumps; discard to make a void in a short non-trump suit; if the Planner's Target is low (3-4), shed off-suit 9s and 8s instead.
- *Crew in play (strategic):* track crew tricks and remaining tricks. Card counting: track which trumps and which high cards of each suit have been played, and treat a card as a "sure winner" if no unplayed card could beat it given the follow-suit rule. Before crew tricks reach the Target, win tricks cheaply (lowest winning card), and prefer that the partner wins if the partner is already winning the trick. Once crew tricks equal the Target, play to lose: the lowest legal card, dump sure winners on tricks the DC or partner is already winning when void, and lead the lowest card of the suit where the DC is most likely to win. At Target + 1 (messy high), play to lose at all costs: one more crew trick blows the job.
- *Double-Crosser (strategic):* estimate the crew's final count from tricks so far plus the crew's sure and likely winners (using the same card counting). If the estimate is at or below the Target, win tricks (push it down); if above, duck and dump high cards (push it up). Re-evaluate each trick.
- *Greedy bot (comparison):* Target = estimate as above; always win a trick if able with the lowest winning card, otherwise play the lowest card.

## 8. Changelog

**v3 (revision 2, 2026-10-04, final loop).** Fixes from playtest-report.md and playtest.json (revision 1, NEEDS-FIXES), critique.md and critique.json (revision 1, REVISE-MAJOR) and panel.json (Bar Raiser veto not active; pet peeves still rulebook weight and ambiguity, so no rules were added). The structure (roles, rotation, 3/6 rounds, deal, Plan, Swap, tricks, bands, tiebreakers) is unchanged.

1. **Crew loot now counts only if the job comes off (clean or messy); a blown job scores the crew 0. The Double-Crosser always scores loot.** This is the critic's required change 1 in its "loot is Double-Crosser-only" form, softened so the crew still compete for loot inside a successful job. *Why:* playtest problem 1 - greedy 63.5% vs strategic 28.5%; ducking cost loot and bought nothing because overshooting was free; doubling/tripling bonuses did not raise strategic. Making over-grabbing cost the grabber's loot attacks that cause directly. *KPI:* mixed table strategic >= 40% and at or above greedy. The critic's flat half-value version is listed as experiment (a) in Design notes so it can be compared.
2. **Clean bonuses raised to +5 (Planner) / +6 (Safecracker); messy set to +2 for both.** *Why:* critic required change 1 (clean +5/+6) - landing exactly must be worth clearly more than landing near; the clean-messy gap is now 3-4 points (was 2). *KPI:* mixed table strategic win rate; clean rate should rise for the strategic bot relative to greedy.
3. **Double-Crosser: blown bonus cut from +5 to +3; messy +1 removed.** *Why:* the DC now also wins on a blown job by wiping out both crew members' loot, so the +5 would over-pay the role; removing the DC messy bonus also makes the DC's payout one line ("always loot, +3 if blown"), easing the panel's rulebook-weight complaint. *KPI:* role points per round within about 1 (v2: 3.84 / 3.53 / 3.95); projected 3.2 / 3.4 / 3.8 before steering improves.
4. **Heat raised from -2 to -3 (not below 0).** *Why:* playtest problem 2 and critic required change 2 - lead changes 1.49 vs target 2; Heat is the lever that moved lead changes most (0.98 without it). Together with change 1 (a blown job now swings a crew member from 6-8 points to 0) this should add volatility. *KPI:* lead changes >= 2; sole-leader-after-round-3 win rate stays <= 65% (v2 52.3%).
5. **Bot hints: card-counting strategic crew and DC described.** *Why:* critic required change 3 - test with a stronger strategic bot so the result is clear either way. *KPI:* a valid strategic-vs-greedy comparison.
6. **Clarifications:** scores can never go negative; tiebreaker 2 counts all tricks won, including those that scored 0 on a blown job. *KPI:* zero rule ambiguities.
7. **Held unchanged on purpose:** rotation, 3/6-round lengths, Targets 3-7, the Swap and the bands. *Why:* seat gap 3.5, contract success 56.6%, runaway 52.3%, length 18.2 min and skill 51.6 all pass, and none of these rules caused the remaining failures. *KPI:* seat gap <= 5 on 4,000+ games; contract success 40-60%; length 16-24 min.

**v2 (revision 1, 2026-10-04).** Fixes from playtest-report.md (NEEDS-FIXES), critique.md (REVISE-MAJOR) and panel.json. panel.json at revision 0 contains no Bar Raiser entry and no active veto flags; the panel's repeated pet peeves (rulebook weight, runaway leaders, ambiguity) are addressed by changes 4-6.

1. **Exact hit replaced by clean / messy / blown bands.** Clean (exact) pays the crew, messy (off by 1) pays the crew a smaller amount and the Double-Crosser +1, blown (off by 2+) pays the Double-Crosser +5. *Why:* playtest problem 1 and critic required change 1 - exact hits were 19.7%, so the DC's +4 was nearly free. *KPI:* contract success (clean or messy) from 19.7% to 40-60%+; DC points per round from 4.8 down to within about 1 point of the other roles.
2. **Bonuses enlarged relative to loot, and a payout for landing exactly.** Clean +3/+4 vs messy +1/+2 means a correctly steered round is worth 2 more points per crew member than a nearly-steered one, and an overshoot or undershoot of 2 costs everything. *Why:* playtest problem 2 - greedy beat strategic 68% to 27% because the contract almost never paid, so only loot mattered. *KPI:* mixed table strategic win rate at or above greedy (target: strategic >= 40%, greedy <= 40%).
3. **Safecracker strengthened: draws and discards 3 (was 2) and takes the bigger cut (+4 clean, +2 messy).** *Why:* playtest problem 3 and critic required change 2 - Safecracker earned 2.7 points per round vs DC 4.8 (win correlation -0.52). *KPI:* Safecracker vs DC points per round within about 1; Safecracker "dominated" flag clears.
4. **Heat now -2 (not below 0), and it is defined when the bonus is 0.** *Why:* playtest problem 4 and critic required change 3 - Heat at -1 cost the leader only about 0.45 points and removing it barely changed anything. Together with larger, more volatile bonuses (change 1) this should create more swings. *KPI:* lead changes from 1.27 to 2 or more; sole-leader-after-round-3 win rate from 64.4% to 60% or less.
5. **No Trump removed.** *Why:* playtest problem 5 and critic required change 4 - chosen 0% of the time; cutting it also shortens the rules. *KPI:* zero dead options; panel rules simplicity up from 0.41.
6. **Rule ambiguities fixed.** Revoke now has one meaning (offender scores 0; contract counted as played). Heat on a 0 bonus has no effect. The role pass is "pass to your right", with the resulting roles spelled out. Tiebreaker 1 counts blown jobs as DC whether or not Heat applied. The deal order is stated. Table talk is limited to "nothing about your hand". *Why:* playtest ambiguities list and critic required change 5. *KPI:* zero rule ambiguities.
7. **Seat balance and kingmaking kept in check without structural changes.** Rotation and 3/6-round lengths are unchanged; every bonus the DC can cause or deny still hits both crew members in the same direction, and the new messy +1 for the DC is paid on the same condition as the crew's messy bonus. *Why:* brief constraints (seat gap <= 5, spoiler must not kingmake) and critic required change 6. *KPI:* seat gap <= 5 points on a 4,000+ game run.
